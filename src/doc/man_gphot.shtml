Growth-Curve Photometry

Growth-curve photometry is primary intended for the absolute photometry calibration. This routine constructs a growth curve of the frame, the enclosed energy of the apertures, by using of machine selected bright isolated stars; it may vary over the field. Total fluxes of all stars are determined from the growth curve and the apertures of the stars, with the errors including the uncertainty of the curve.

Synopsis

munipack gphot [.. parameters ..] file(s)[,result(s)]

Description

Growth-curve photometry determines total fluxes (GCOUNT) of all star-like objects of frames processed by aperture photometry: the sums of counts from a star in predefined apertures (APERPHOT). The frames need many apertures from about the FWHM to several FWHM, all inside the sky ring, for example aphot --apertures 1,1.313,1.7241,2.2637,2.9724,3.9028,5.1245,6.7287,8.8349,11.601,15.232,20,23,26.4,30.4,35,40 --ring 45,60.

The growth curve

The growth curve is the enclosed energy EEk of the apertures, the fraction of the total flux in the aperture k, determined exactly as the automatic aperture correction of aphot (aphot --apcorr auto): bright (S/N > 30, at least the 30 brightest), unsaturated stars without a neighbour brighter than 1% within 5.5 FWHM (of FIND), whose apertures inside the sky ring are valid (apertures contaminated by brighter neighbours are invalid); the total flux is the mean of three plateau apertures, the first window from 1.3 FWHM whose slope relative to the total is consistent with zero, searched up to twice the radius enclosing 99% of the stacked curve (keys EERTOT1, EERTOT2, EEPLAT, EER99, EERCAP). EE of every aperture is a polynomial in x,y (or in the distance from the field centre, --radial) of the order raised from zero while significant, up to --var; the errors of the counts are those of nested apertures.

The errors of EE are the errors of a prediction for the stars which did not make the curve: the covariance of the polynomial and the scatter of the stars over it are inflated (by EELAMk) so that the stars predicted without themselves (leave one out) scatter as their errors say, and the stars outside the range of the curve's stars add the uncertainty of the extrapolation. On frames with few stars and a PSF varying over the field the variation is often not significant, and the errors then describe it.

The total flux

The counts of the apertures of a star are Ck = GCOUNT · EEk + noise, the covariance of the noise is that of nested apertures (they share their pixels and the error of the mean sky): Cov(Ci,Cj) = Vp(Cmin(i,j)) + Ai Aj SKYERR², Vp the sky and read noise of the pixels and the photon noise of the star by the model (the gain APERRGN of APERPHOT). GCOUNT is the estimate of the least variance among the single apertures Ck/EEk and the best linear unbiased estimates by the apertures 1 … m (up to the end of the plateau); its variance includes the error of EE (bounded as fully correlated between the apertures). Faint stars get the sum of the apertures weighted by the profile, with the precision of PSF photometry; bright stars the larger apertures, where EE is known better than their photon noise. GCOUNTERR is the standard deviation of GCOUNT.

GCOUNT is the flux within the plateau apertures (EERTOT1 … EERTOT2), the total flux of aphot's APCOUNT/APEE: the light of a PSF beyond the plateau is a constant fraction absorbed by the calibration.

SKY of GROWPHOT is the sky of APERPHOT plus a residual sky b estimated by the apertures of the star with the prior 0 ± SKYERR of the ring (the error of the mean of the ring is common to all apertures); its SKYERR is the posterior error. The apertures hold the light of faint neighbours, so SKY is higher than the true sky in crowded fields (by 0.3 SKYERR on the simulated dense fields, 0.6 with Moffat wings and 40 px plateau apertures).

Versions before 0.6.2+hg1653 (patched) fitted the growth curve by a model of profiles and selected its stars by HWHM; on frames with a PSF of a sharp core and a halo the curve was built from a few stars and no star had a valid GCOUNT, and the errors of bright stars missed the error of the curve (1.4–1.8 times too small). The old method is --legacy (bit-identical results).

Output

The extension GROWPHOT (X, Y, SKY, SKYERR, GCOUNT, GCOUNTERR, GROWFLAG: 1 for the stars of the curve; HWHM and RADFLX90 of the curve, GROWMETH and the EE* keys of the curve as in APERPHOT of aphot --apcorr auto) and the extension GROWCURVE (R, GROW, GROWERR: the median EE of the stars and its error, RPROF: the mean surface brightness of the annuli relative to the total flux). Stars without a valid GCOUNT have −1.

Parameters

--var
The highest order of the polynomial of the spatial variation of the growth curve, 0, 1 or 2 (default 2); the order is raised from zero while significant, and lowered for few stars.
--radial
The variation by the distance from the field centre (default: x,y), for PSFs varying with the field radius.
--centre
x,y: the field centre of --radial [pix] (default: NAXIS/2).
--legacy
The growth-curve fit of the versions before.
-th, --threshold
With --legacy: the stars of the curve are brighter than the threshold times the sky in their aperture (default 0.1).
--verbose
With this (common) parameter, the growth curve and the apertures of GCOUNT are printed.

Examples

Common usage

$ munipack gphot --verbose pleiades.fits
$ munipack gphot --radial frame.fits
$ munipack phcal --photometry grow -c catalogue.fits ... frame.fits
