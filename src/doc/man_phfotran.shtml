Photometric System Transformation

The transformation of an instrumental set of filters to a standard one is determined on a field with known calibration sources: colour terms of the magnitudes (the default), or the photon fluxes of the instrumental filters as a linear combination of the standard ones.

Synopsis

munipack phfotran [.. parameters ..] file(s)

Description

A common astronomical apparatus composed from a telescope, filter and a detector has slightly different spectral sensitivity than the standard one which had established the primary (stellar) standards of a photometric system. Fortunately, a commonly used equipment close fits the standard spectral sensitivity due to effort of manufactures. Therefore, any differences are small and can be, with suitable precision, approximated by a linear approximation.

This action determines such transformation by application of the linear approximation between observed sum of counts and expected photons from calibration stars. Two models are available (--model).

Magnitude model (--model mag, default)

The standard transformation of every filter n by the colour of the standard magnitudes of the filter and its neighbour:

mstd − minst = ZP + k1 (c − cref) [+ k2 (c − cref)², --color-order 2],

minst = −2.5 log10 of the count rate [counts/s/m²], c = mn−1 − mn in the order of the photometry system, for the first filter m1 − m2 (B − V for B and V, V − R for R, R − I for I of Johnson BVRI), cref the mean colour of the stars (the coefficients nearly uncorrelated with ZP). The fit is that of the colour term of phcal: the mixture likelihood with the systematic error (outliers without weight), the errors of the colours propagated with their covariance with the magnitude of the filter, the errors of the coefficients with the uncertainty of the systematic error (phcal). With --verbose the coefficients, the systematic error, the fraction of outliers and the scatter of the stars without and with the colour terms are printed.

phcal --tratab fits CTPH of every filter with the coefficients fixed (by the catalogue colours of the calibration stars) and gives every star the standard magnitudes solved from its calibrated magnitudes in all filters, with the errors of the coefficients in MAGERR.

Flux model (--model flux)

The transformation relates the photon rates p [ph/s/m²] of the stars in the standard filters (by the catalogue magnitudes, PHOTSYS2) and their count rates c [counts/s/m²] in the instrumental filters (PHOTSYS1) linearly:

ci = Σj Tij pj, i,j = { B,V ...}

The forward matrix T (photons to counts) holds the efficiency of the instrument on its diagonal and the colour terms off it: an instrumental filter shifted to the red sees a part of the photons of the next standard filter. The colour terms of the extinction (of a broad passband) are of the same kind. The backward matrix (counts to photons) is fitted independently.

phcal --tratab uses the forward matrix of a flux table for the calibration (the expected counts of the calibration stars, CTPH is then a correction factor near 1 for the same instrument) and its inverse to convert the counts of all stars to the photons of the standard filters, so the magnitudes have no colour term. A frame of a filter needs the frames of the neighbouring filters for the colours: stars missing on one of them have no calibrated values, and the images are converted by the diagonal only (by CTPH with the magnitude model).

The choice of the model

The magnitude model is the default: it is the transformation of the standard photometry (Landolt, Stetson), its coefficients are those quoted for instruments and comparable with them, and a passband shifted or reshaped against the standard one gives colour terms nearly linear in magnitudes. The flux model is exact for a passband that is a linear combination of the standard ones (a leak of a neighbouring band, a red leak) and approximates the colour terms linear in magnitudes to 0.013 mag rms over two magnitudes of B − V; the magnitude model approximates a strong mixing (10–13%) only with --color-order 2 (see Validation).

The transformation is designed to be used on a calibration field. The sparse field with many of well calibrated stars. There are sources of such fields (which can be supposed as the secondary standards):

  • Stetson's fields

Algorithm

The transformation is determined by the way:

  • Reference catalogue and frames are merged in spherical coordinates with nearly positions.
  • From known filter in a photometric system and catalogue magnitude, photon fluxes are derived.
  • Observed counts are normalized to rates using of both telescope area and exposure time.
  • The transformation matrix is determined.

The magnitude model is fitted filter by filter (see above). In the flux model, a result of the transformation is a nearly tri-diagonal matrix (elements around diagonal dominates over other ones). The limitation of the shape is forced due to ill-conditioning of the problem. The filters are taken in the order of the photometry system (wavelengths); the row of the reference filter of the system (FILTREF, V for Johnson) is fitted against the colour of its neighbour, c/p = TVV + TVB pB/pV (or of both neighbours, a plane), the other filters through the ratios of the counts of neighbouring filters against the ratios of their photons (robust line fits).

Two filters (B,V, no leak of R): −0.164 → 0.0000 ± 0.0002. Colour terms linear in magnitudes (B' = B + 0.1 (B − V)) are approximated by the photon mixing to 0.013 mag rms over two magnitudes of B − V. Input And Output

On input, FITS frames in several filters (with the instrumental photometry and astrometry, as for phcal) are required, one frame per filter. The frames are paired with the filters (-f) and the catalogue columns (--col-mag, --col-magerr, in the order of -f) by their FILTER keyword, in any order. Composited frames are recommended.

On output, a new FITS table representing the transformation is created (-o, phfotran.fits by default; format). It contains the data of the fit too: a table given as the only file (without a catalogue) is fitted again.

Parameters

-c, --cat
reference catalogue (as phcal)
-f, --filters
filters, one per frame
--col-ra, --col-dec, --col-mag, --col-magerr
columns of the catalogue; the magnitudes in the order of -f
--model
mag (colour terms of the magnitudes, default) or flux (the linear mixing of photon rates)
--color-order
the order of the colour terms of the magnitude model, 1 (default) or 2
--photsys-ref
the standard photometry system of the catalogue (PHOTSYS2)
--photsys-instr
the name of the instrumental system (PHOTSYS1; default: PHOTSYS of the frames); phcal checks it against its --photsys-instr or the frames
-C, --cal
factors multiplying the counts of the filters (in the order of -f)
-E, --extin
extinction coefficients k of the filters: the counts are multiplied by exp(k X), X the airmass of the frame (the transformation outside the atmosphere)
--area
the area of the telescope [m²]
--tol
search radius for the cross-match [deg]
--phsystab, --list
the table of photometry systems, list of them

Caveats

Just equal number of instrumental and standard filters is implemented. Stars are selected by fixed limits (signal to noise above 5, relative error below 0.1). The front end did not run phfotran before (the action was listed, but not dispatched); the utility itself crashed at the end of its reading of the frames (BUGS P45). The default model is the magnitude one: for the matrix of the earlier versions, give --model flux. The colours of the magnitude model are fixed (a filter and its neighbour).

Examples

The transformation of an instrument by the standard field T Phe and its use: 

$ munipack phfotran --area 1.86 --photsys-instr DK154 --photsys-ref Johnson \
   -c T_Phe.fits --col-ra RA --col-dec DEC -f B,V,R,I --col-mag B,V,R,I \
   T_Phe_000001.fits T_Phe_000003.fits T_Phe_000005.fits T_Phe_000007.fits
$ munipack phcal --tratab phfotran.fits --photsys-instr DK154 --photsys-ref Johnson \
   -c T_Phe.fits --col-ra RA --col-dec DEC -f B,V,R,I --col-mag B,V,R,I \
   T_Phe_000001.fits T_Phe_000003.fits T_Phe_000005.fits T_Phe_000007.fits
