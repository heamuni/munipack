Astrometry

An astrometry calibration of FITS frames.

Synopsis

munipack astrometry [.. parameters ..] file(s)[,result(s)]

Description

Astrometry action derives astrometry calibration of FITS frames (Overview).

Astrometry is naturally separated on two parts:

  • Matching where the correspondence between stars on frame and in catalogue is established. The matching can be done without knowledge of any transformation, when very general presumptions are supposed (the coordinates are Euclidean).
  • Astrometry transformation is computed by using of robust algorithms.

Reccomendations

The matching process is searching for a mutual correspondece in two list of coordinates. The mathcing is generaly slow and complex process. To increase of its reliability and speed, is is important to compare (and prepare) the list by such way so that the coordinates as well as magnitues overlaps. The overlap in coodinates means to use of catalogues selection with very similar centre and radius as the frames. Also magnitude overlap must corresponds with non-saturated (and non-faint) stars on images. (If both the conditions will not meet, the matching will probbaly also sucessufll.)

There two ways how to realise. The coordinate correspondece can be make with known object position and field of view (FOV) of telescope (it means angular diameter of the cone on sky) in degress. If the telescope has FOV 0.5 deg and the observed objects is Crab nebulae than the optimal selection from UCAC4 cataloguje is: 

  munipack cone -r 0.2 -- 83.63 22.01

The magnitude selection is simliar. It depends on all telescope, exposure time and filter. The mathcing (as well as photometry) will sucesfull only with non-saturated bright stars. If we have an experinece that the stars are saturated at 12, than the catalogue selection can be improved (syntax is column_name=[some constrain]): 

  munipack cone -r 0.2 --par 'f.mag=>12' -- 83.63 22.01

Alternatively, the filtration capabailities of FITSIO can be used 

  munipack astrometry -c 'cone.fits[1][$f.mag$>12]' crab.fits

We are selecting only non-saturated stars fainter than magnitude 12 in column f.mag. The usual linear range of 16-bit CCD cameras is about 3-5 magnitudes.

Parameter --maxmatch slices the catalogue on parts with similar magnitudes (magnitudes in certain range): both the catalogue and the frame are sorted by brightness (the catalogue by --col-mag, the frame by the counts of the first aperture of APERPHOT; the tables can be in any order), the brightest --maxstars (250) of each are used, in chunks of --maxmatch (42) stars: the chunks of the frame (three at most) are matched against the chunks of the catalogue. Versions before 0.6.2+hg1653 (patched) took the first rows of the tables as they were: the catalogue in the order of the file (a cone search not sorted by magnitude gave the first 250 rows of the cone, often few of them on a small frame), the frame in the order of the detection. The catalogue is read whole: when the pointing of a frame is known (its WCS, or --rcen, --dcen with a scale), its match takes the brightest --maxstars of the catalogue stars near the frame (within the half-diagonal of the frame with 10% and the tolerance of the offset --wcs-offset), so a cone much larger than the frame can be used (versions before took the brightest 250 of the whole cone, few of them on the frame).

Parameter --minmatch sets minimal length of match sequence which is supposet to by complete (sucesfull match). For sparse fileds, the defaults are usually satisfactyory, but very dense fields like SMC, LMC needs increase this parameters. Munipack sets the default of 5, which is also the minimum. The small values on crowded fiedls can produce false match (because probability of miss-match is getting appreciable).

Algorithm

Lets we denote the reference star coordinates as the catalogue and coordinates of detected stars as the frame.

Guided match (the WCS or the pointing)

When the frame has an astrometry calibration in its header (a WCS with CD or CDELT and PC: one by the telescope, a previous run, or an approximate one by hand), or when its pointing is given by --rcen, --dcen (the centre of the frame, or --xcen, --ycen) with the scale by --scale or by the WCS, the match mode first uses it (unless --blind is given); the rotation is that of the WCS or of --angle, the reflection that of the WCS or of --reflex. When the rotation is known (the WCS, --angle), the catalogue stars are predicted on the frame by the calibration, the predictions are rotated and scaled on a grid within --wcs-rot (3° by default) and --wcs-scale (±3%), and for every node of the grid the differences of the positions of the 60 brightest frame stars and of the brightest catalogue stars near the frame vote for the offset (in bins of 4 × --sig pixels, within --wcs-offset, by default 10% of the larger side of the frame and 100 pixels at least). The node and the offset of the most votes give the transformation, the nearest stars by it are the match, and the solution is fitted and tested (the significance, with the trials of the vote: the nodes and the bins) as any other (ASTRMETH = WCS).

When the rotation (the roll) is unknown, or wrong: without a WCS and --angle, with --roll-unknown (or --wcs-rot 180), or when the match within --wcs-rot fails, any rotation is tried (ASTRMETH = POINTING). The grid of the whole circle would need thousands of nodes (the step moves the stars at the edge of the frame by a bin: 0.08° on 4096², 4550 rotations, times the scales), so the candidates are found first by the pairs of stars, which do not depend on the rotation and the offset: every pair of the 30 brightest frame stars (15% of the frame long at least) and every pair of the brightest catalogue stars within the circle of the frame, the scale and the offset of the same length (within --wcs-scale and the bin) give the rotation (both orientations) and the offset (by the midpoints), which vote in the cells of 0.5–4° and 16–25 pixels; the true pairs vote for one cell (n(n−1)/2 pairs of n common stars), the others are spread over the circle. The eight windows (2 × 2 × 2 cells) of the most votes are the candidates; the vote above is done around each of them (its window, the scales), the most votes give the transformation, the nearest stars the match. The significance counts the trials of the whole search (the circle at the step of the grid, the scales, the offsets: 2·107 on 768², 9·109 on 4096² with the default offset), so the frames of five or six stars need the offset not much larger than it is. --reflex-unknown tries both reflections (the trials doubled). The order of the guided match is: the rotation known within --wcs-rot (when the WCS or --angle gives it; not with --roll-unknown), any rotation (not with --roll-known), then the blind match below (with the scale prior by the header or --scale): first of the catalogue stars near the frame by the pointing (within the half-diagonal of the frame with 10% and the tolerance of the offset), then of the brightest of all catalogue stars. The keyword ASTRMETH of the header records the method of the solution: WCS (guided, the rotation known), POINTING (guided, any rotation), BLIND, CROSS or SEQUENCE.

QUVIK-like simulated frames (512² to 4096², 0.756″/pixel, M67, M35, the field at RA 180°, Dec 0° and a dark cloud) with the WCS in the header moved by 30–100 pixels, rotated by 1–3° and scaled by 1–3%: all solved by the WCS-guided match (the same solutions as by the blind match), in a few hundredths of a second on frames up to 2048², 0.5 s on 4096².

QUVIK-like frames of any roll (768² to 4096², random rolls, the same fields; 32 frames, 26 of five stars and more): the header (or --rcen, --dcen, --scale) moved by 10″–5′, rotated by a random angle, scaled by 1–2%, the catalogue of the believed footprint with 10% and the offset on each side: all frames of five stars and more but one (6 stars on the frame, 20 in the catalogue: also not solved by the blind match) solved; the offsets within the default tolerance by the guided match of any rotation, the others by the blind match near the frame; the median times by one thread 0.03 s (768²), 0.04 s (1024²), 0.1 s (2048²), 0.17–0.26 s (4096²), the any-rotation pass 0.001–0.09 s of them, the errors of the solutions against the truth 0.03–0.08 pixels rms (median), as by the blind match. The true field moved by 15–50% of the frame (the catalogue padded by the shift and 10%) with --wcs-offset by the shift: 80 of 84 frames by the guided match (a frame of six stars shifted by 50% is rejected as not significant, by the trials of the larger search, 2·1010; with the default offset the blind match solves it), 0.04–1.4 s. Against the catalogues of other fields at the same place, with --roll-unknown --reflex-unknown --wcs-offset 55%: none of 96 accepted (none of 96 by the default search either).

Recommended settings: the pointing and the scale known, the roll unknown

For a frame whose pointing is known to tens of arcseconds (minutes at worst), whose scale is known to 1–2%, and whose rotation is unknown (QUVIK-like): 

munipack astrometry -c cone.fits --rcen RA --dcen DEC --scale 4761.9 \
     [--wcs-offset 3arcmin] [--wcs-scale 0.02] frame.fits

(or the approximate WCS in the header, any rotation in it, without --rcen, --dcen). The rotation is unknown without a WCS and --angle; with a WCS of a rotation that may be wrong, --roll-unknown skips the useless pass of the rotation within 3° (0.1 s on 4096²). Set --wcs-offset to the uncertainty of the pointing (with units: 90arcsec, 3arcmin, 20% of the frame; by default 10% of the frame, 100 pixels at least: 76″ at 0.756″/pixel; at most the diagonal of the frame); a pointing beyond it is still solved by the blind match of the catalogue stars near the frame. A catalogue cone larger than the frame is fine (its stars near the pointing are used). Use --reflex-unknown only when the reflection (the parity of the instrument) is not known: --reflex sets it.

Blind match

The following algorithm is used for matching:

  • Catalogue coordinates are projected to rectangular. The projection can be set with -p,--projection switch. As the centre of projection all stars computed as arithmetical mean is selected.
  • Frame coordinates are pre-scaled to lie in interval 0 .. 1 and to have origin in centre of frame (the projected and measured coordinates has similar scale and origin after this transformation to improve numerical precision).
  • Twines of stars in catalogue and in frame are get as the starting step of matching algorithm.
  • To get a next star in sequence, the following function (in a meta-language) illustrates the principle: 
    Function Sequence for next star
  For all unused stars in catalogue:
     Compute u1,v1
     For all unused stars on image:
        Compute u2,v1
        Is Acceptable and distance({u1,u2} and {v1,v2}) < limit?
            Has the sequence required length?
              Got Solution!
            Call Sequence for next+1 star
            Did fail the Sequence?
              Skip the star
End function Sequence
    Variables u1,v1 and u2,v2 are coordinates of in a triangle space of a triplet of stars. The distance is distance in triangle space.
  • Implemented algorithm includes additional tests to be sequence acceptable:
    • the sequence length and limit for objects used to matching can be changed by --minmatch, --maxmatch parameters
    • The limit is set via --sig parameter which sets σ expected error in the coordinates of stars. The parameter is also used for estimation of tolerances of angles.
    • Angles computed between all stars in a real space must be inside tolerance.
    • the sequence must have first and last stars connected (all implemented tests must be passed).
    • To prevent use of degenerated triangles (for sides a+b≈c), only triangles with the v > 1 + σ - u are used.
  • The first sequence, which is successful found, stops matching. When --full-match is used, it is scan throughout all possible twines. In this case, the sequence with the best parameters is used. The full scan can take a lot of time.
  • The pairs of the chunks of the frame and of the catalogue are matched by parallel threads (OpenMP): the match is that of the first pair of chunks in their order which succeeds, the pairs after it are abandoned, so the solution is the same for any number of threads (with the same trials for the significance). The number of threads is OMP_NUM_THREADS; by default the CPUs available to the process (its affinity and the CPU quota of its control group: docker run --cpus 2 gives 2 threads, the OpenMP runtime alone would take all cores of the host) divided by the parallel processes (--mproc); --verbose prints it. The blind match without a solution, or with it in a later chunk, is faster: a wrong sky of a dense 2048² frame without a scale prior takes 54 s by one thread, 28, 15 and 9–11 s by 2, 4 and 8 threads; the 30 full cones without a WCS 29 s in all, 20, 18 and 15.5 s.

An alternative sequence can be constructed from known transformation and the astrometry is just more precise.

The known sequence of stars can be used to determine astrometry transformation:

  • The coordinates of catalogue stars are corrected for its proper motion to the epoch of the frame (DATE-OBS) from the epoch of the catalogue (the keyword JEPOCH of the table, or the columns --col-epoch-ra, --col-epoch-dec). The proper motion in RA is the arc on the sky, μα cos δ (pmRA of UCAC4, UCAC5, Gaia, SDSS in VizieR); the change of RA is pmRA / cos δ (versions before 0.6.2+hg1653 (patched) added pmRA to RA: wrong by the factor 1/cos δ, 2 at Dec 60°). When the catalogue has proper motions but no epoch, they are ignored with a warning (on the standard error output and a COMMENT in the header).
  • Initial estimation of parameters is computed by minimising of absolute deviations.
  • The corresponding stars in catalogue and frame are found by searching for stars in close neighbourhood (practically, the --match nearly is performed.
  • The transformation is determined with all possibles stars by the robust minimisation.
  • The final fit (TAN, ten stars and more) is weighted by the errors of the centroids: the error of a star is estimated by its S/N in the first aperture of APERPHOT and the width of the PSF (HWHM of APERPHOT or FWHM of FIND), with an excess scatter (the errors of the catalogue, of the centroids of bright stars) by the maximum likelihood of the residuals; the weights apply within the robust function. QUVIK-like frames (36 solutions of 10–220 stars): the rms error of the solution over the frame against the truth 0.099 → 0.055 pixels (median), the maximal error 0.17 → 0.10 pixels, better in 22 frames, worse in 2. The TPV deformation (-p TPV) is fitted without the weights.

Significance of the solution

A match can be false: on a dense field, or against a catalogue of another part of the sky, a sequence of five or more stars that fulfils all the tests is found by chance (the search tries millions of pairs of stars). The solution is therefore tested against chance coincidences: the stars whose residuals are within r = 3 × the scatter of the fit (at least --sig, at most the limit of the cross-match) are counted; two of them fix the transformation and match by construction, the others are compared with the Poisson distribution of the coincidences of the frame stars with the catalogue stars, whose density is that in the overlap of the catalogue and the frame. The probability is multiplied by the number of the transformations tried by the match (the starting pairs of the sequences: the trials) and the solution is accepted when it is below 10−3. The --verbose output shows the numbers (significance). The test is disabled by --disable-rms-check.

On QUVIK-like simulated frames (512² to 2048², 5 to 430 stars) against catalogues of other parts of the sky (88 runs) the test rejected all the false solutions (the previous test, which counted all cross-matched stars including the matched sequence, without the trials, by the density in the bounding box of the frame stars, accepted 4 of them, with scales of 13–18″/pixel for the true 0.76″/pixel); the probability of the true solutions was below 10−9, also with five or six stars.

The calibration needs at least five matched stars: the solution has four parameters (the scale, the rotation and the centre) and their errors (the match sequence is five stars at least, --minmatch smaller than five is rejected). A frame with fewer stars on it (or in the catalogue) fails with the error too few stars, and a solution which cross-matches fewer than five stars is unreliable; the other frames of the same run are processed (the exit status is 1 when any frame fails). There is no solution from fewer stars (versions before 0.6.2+hg1653 (patched) documented an alternative with two stars, which did not work: it stopped, or crashed on the residuals of a 'low-precision solution', the whole run with it). Generally, the astrometry has recommended minimum of 7 stars, but ideal is over 20, and sparse frames (5–10 stars) need a catalogue restricted to the field of view (and the significance test needs a few stars beyond the two fixing the solution). Any modern (like Munipack default UCAC5) catalogue has more reference objects on medium crowded fields.

Prerequisites

The calibration needs both detected stars and instrumental photometry.

Input And Output

On input, list of frames containing the table with already detected stars and photometry is expected.

On output, the WCS calibration in the header of primary FITS is created (or updated) for all input images.

Distortions (TPV)

With -p TPV the gnomonic projection is completed by the TPV polynomials of the third order (PV1_0 … PV1_11, PV2_0 … PV2_11, including the radial terms), fitted robustly. The fit is staged: the gnomonic solution is found first and the stars are cross-matched by it within the limit --sig × 5 (or 5 × its scatter); a deformation of the second order is fitted on them, all stars are cross-matched again by that solution (within 5 × the larger of --sig and the scatter of the fit) and the fit is repeated until the cross-matched set does not change; then the third order is fitted and iterated in the same way. A large distortion moves the stars at the edges of the field beyond the limit of the gnomonic solution, and a polynomial fitted only on the inner stars is extrapolated there; the iterations recover them. The progress is printed with --verbose (TPV stage).

Synthetic 2048² fields (1″/pixel, 0.05 pixel centroid noise, three random patterns of quadratic and cubic terms, rotation 0° and 30°, mirrored or not): with the distortion of 15 pixels after the best affine fit, the maximal error at the stars of the frame was 0.2–5.7 pixels with the default 250 stars and 0.1–60 pixels with --maxstars 1000 (edge stars not matched, or matched to wrong stars) in versions before 0.6.2+hg1653 (patched); now 0.06–0.11 and 0.03–0.07 pixels, the same as without distortion. Undistorted frames give the same solution as before.

Caveats

The atmospheric refraction is not modelled (the TPV polynomials absorb its differential part on small fields).

Examples

Sources Of Astrometric Catalogues

Virtual Observatory (VO) is intended as the main source of catalogues. The following example uses cone search capability of VO to list part of UCAC5 catalogue 

munipack cone -c UCAC5 -o 0716cat.fits -r 0.1 -- 110.25 71.34

UCAC5 is the recommended astrometric catalogue (default). We must provide coordinates of centre of frame (use coordinates of assumed object) and the cone radius (else a catalogue default will be used). Note parameter -s (sort by a column). The astrometry sorts the catalogue by the magnitude column itself (--col-mag), so the sorting of the cone search is not needed any more; a cone restricted to the field of view of the frame (with a margin of about 10%) speeds up the matching and makes it more reliable.

Manual Calibration

Manual calibration is designed with its properties:

  • The proper calibration is known.
  • No catalogue and objects detection on images is required.

To save a calibration to FITS header, find the parameters by hand and adjust the example 

$ munipack astrometry -m manual -p TAN --rcen=110.471 --dcen=71.351
            --scale=5.64e-04 --angle=0.0 0716_1R.fits

This is an example for blazar 0716+71 which observation is in FITS file 0716_1R.fits included to munipack-blazar.tar.gz.

The calibration parameters are left untouched. There are are no limits and no any checks.

Matching And Sequence Calibration

Astrometric calibration is designed with its properties:

  • High precision as possible.
  • Minimum of manually provided parameters

Prerequisites

Astrometry calibration requires detected stars (see find), aperture photometry (see aphot) and an astrometry catalogue (cone search).

At the start, astrometry calibration itself can be done, a star table (list) of objects on an image must be prepared.

The object detection (as a side effect is the aperture photometry) is relative straightforward (detected objects are stored to another HDU with label FIND): 

$ munipack find -f 2 -t 5  0716_1R.fits
$ munipack aphot 0716_1R.fits

A catalogue with reference stars can be got with help of Virtual Observatory: 

$ munipack cone -o 0716_cat.fits -r 0.2 -- 110.25 71.34

Selected astrometric stars in radius 0.2° around centre α = 110.25° and δ = 71.34° are saved to the FITS table 0716_cat.fits.

Note selection of catalogue UCAC4. The objects in the output file are sorted by magnitude designed as 'f.mag'. You must known catalogue structure before use of -s (simply get catalogue without -s option, look for structure and than use -s with the given magnitude column label). The astrometry sorts the catalogue by --col-mag itself.

$ munipack astrometry -c 0716_cat.fits  0716_1R.fits

Invoking of Sequence

  $ munipack astrometry -m SEQUENCE \
     --seq1 5,6,9,16,17,18,19,21,23,22,24,26,27,29,28,30,32 \
     --seq2 1,2,3,5,4,8,11,13,7,10,6,12,9,18,14,19,17 \
     -c 0716_cat.fits  0716_1R.fits

[†] Parameters --minmatch and --maxmatch affects both speed (larger is slower) and chance of matching (lower is worse). The recommended values for --minmatch are 5 - 10 (default and minimum 5) and --maxmatch 20 - 200 (default 42).

Tips For Usage

Successful Matching

Matching needs at least --minmatch common stars on frame and catalogue as selected --maxmatch. When matching is failed, check all centre, radius and proper reference catalogue.

As the best diagnostics tool is the astrometry in xmunipack (Tools->Astrometry).

Fine Tune Of Calibration

The matching of the reference catalogue with detected stars is extremely complicated procedure. Therefore, there are tun-able parameters for both matching and fitting algorithms. In doubts, check ones:

  • The magnitude column of the catalogue (--col-mag): both the catalogue and the detected stars are sorted by brightness internally, the brightest of them are matched.
  • The search region is overlapping of the field of view of matched pictures.
  • Try increase number of stars used for matching (--maxmatch) and a match sequence length (--minmatch). Large values of both parameters slow-downs matching.
