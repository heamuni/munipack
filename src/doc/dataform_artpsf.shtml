Artificial PSF format

This document describes the format of a point spread function (PSF) FITS file intended for simulation in Artificial sky tool (manual) and for PSF photometry. The PSF may vary over the field of view.

Structure

The primary header contains the keyword

ZOOM
(integer) the number of PSF samples per pixel along each axis.

The keywords RGRID and AGRID of older files are not used.

Every image extension having both keywords

RADCOO
[deg] the angular distance from the field centre,
ANGCOO
[deg] the position angle, atan2(y,x) of the pixel offsets from the field centre (counter-clockwise from the x-axis, any value, taken modulo 360)

is the PSF at the field position. The extension with RADCOO = 0 is the PSF at the centre. The PSFs with an equal RADCOO form a ring; the number of rings and the number of angles on each ring are arbitrary and the angles of different rings may differ. The order of extensions is not important. Extensions without both keywords are ignored.

A file without such extensions, with a two-dimensional primary image and ZOOM, is a single PSF valid in the whole field (the older format).

PSF

The PSF is a two-dimensional array of real values: the intensity sampled with ZOOM samples per pixel (any size, the sizes of extensions may differ). The sample at the 1-based pixel (CRPIX1, CRPIX2) of the extension, when present, otherwise at (NAXIS1/2, NAXIS2/2) (integer division), is the centre of the star: the star is placed at its catalogue position by this sample. (The template generator below and the generator of field-dependent PSFs place the centre there.)

Normalisation: the sum of the samples should be ZOOM² (e.g. 4 for ZOOM = 2); a star then gets its whole flux. The data are used as they are: a sum s gives the fraction s/ZOOM² of the flux (the light outside the stamp can be modelled this way), a warning is printed when the sum differs from ZOOM² by more than 0.1%.

Interpolation over the field

The position of a star in the field is given by its pixel offsets (x,y) from the field centre (the pixel NAXIS/2 of the frame, integer division, which is the reference pixel CRPIX of artificial frames), the radius is r = √(x²+y²) / scale, where scale is in pixels per degree, the angle is atan2(y,x).

  1. On every ring, the PSF is interpolated linearly in the angle between the two neighbouring PSFs (periodically over 360°; a ring with a single PSF is constant).
  2. Inside the first ring, the PSF is interpolated linearly in the radius between the centre PSF and the first ring.
  3. Between two rings, it is interpolated linearly in the radius.
  4. Beyond the outermost ring, the outermost ring is used. (Without the centre PSF, the first ring is used inside of it.)

Pixelisation

The image of a star in detector pixels is the integral over the pixels of the interpolant of the samples: linear between samples for even ZOOM, constant in the cells of samples for odd ZOOM. The integral is exact for any sub-pixel position of the star and it conserves the flux (the sum of pixels is the sum of samples / ZOOM²). For a star at a position on the sample grid, it is the trapezoid rule (even ZOOM) or the midpoint rule (odd ZOOM) over the samples in a pixel. The analytic PSFs of artificial and psfphot are sampled with ZOOM = 16 and pixelised in the same way.

Empirical PSF files

psfphot --psf EMPIRICAL --psf-save file writes the empirical PSF of a frame in this format: a single PSF at the centre (RADCOO = 0) for a constant PSF, or the PSFs on rings around the field centre up to the farthest corner of the frame (2 rings by 16 angles for the variation of the order 0 or 1 or for a corrected PSF file, 12 rings by 32 angles for the order 2). The stamps are centred at CRPIX1, CRPIX2 of each extension and sum to ZOOM² (the core is sampled out to 99.9% of its flux). The primary header has also PSFCEN1, PSFCEN2 (the field centre [pix] used for RADCOO, ANGCOO), PSFSCALE (the scale [pix/deg]; 1 when the frame has no WCS, RADCOO is then in pixels and the file is used with --scale 1), FWHM and PSFRAD [pix]. The field centre is NAXIS/2 of the frame (as for artificial), so the file applies to frames of the same size and scale.

Limitations

  • Data arrays are two-dimensional.
  • ZOOM should by an integer number, the same for all PSFs.
  • Non-integer CRPIX is rounded to the nearest sample.
