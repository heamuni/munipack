Averaged dark frame

Compute an average dark frame of a set of dark frames.

Synopsis

munipack dark [-bias file] [--arith|--hampel] [..] file(s)

Description

A dark frame is a map of a dark current, thermally generated charged particles captured in pixels. Amount of the dark current is proportional both temperature and exposure time. This routine creates an average dark frame, sometimes named as a masterdark.

An output image Oij is computed as the average of every pixel of input images (Ik)ij:

Oij = 〈Ik - B〉ij,

where i,j is an index of a pixel and k is an index of an image. The bias frame Bij is optional. When one is available, one is subtracted from dark exposures before averaging.

The average 〈 〉 is the mean clipped by the noise model of the frames (the same routine as the sky of aphot and the mean of kombine): the mean of the values of a pixel within t − 5σ … t + q + √(q² + (5σ)²), iterated from the median, where t is the mean, σ the larger of the scatter of the values and the noise of a value by the noise model of its frame, √((t − b)/GAIN + RN²) [ADU], and q = 25/6 photo-electrons (the Bernstein bound of the Poisson tail: no electron of a dark of a fraction of an electron per pixel is clipped, while cosmic rays and other outliers, many σ above, are). The noise model: GAIN of the frame (without it, σ is the scatter alone and q = 25/6 ADU), the read noise RN by READNS [ADU] or RDNOISE [e-], and the level of no electrons b: zero with the bias frame subtracted, the pedestal BIASLEV of the frame (artificial), zero when unknown (the noise of a raw frame with a pedestal is then overestimated and the clipping of outliers looser; the mean is not biased: use -bias). The robust mean used before (--hampel) is biased low on dark frames of a few electrons per pixel, a skewed, discrete (Poisson) distribution. Ten QUVIK-like frames (gain 0.101 e-/ADU, read noise 0.37 e-, 12 masters of 256² pixels), the master − the true dark: 0.05 e-: −0.0114 (−23%) by the robust mean, +0.0001 ± 0.0002 e-/pixel by the clipped mean; 0.13 e-: −0.023 (−18%), +0.0003 ± 0.0002; 2 e-: −0.048 (−2.4%), −0.0007 ± 0.0005; 20 e-: −0.051 (−0.25%), −0.001 ± 0.003. With 20 cosmic rays per frame the clipped mean is the same and no cosmic-ray pixel is left (--arith: +0.68 e-/pixel). At high counts (10⁴ e-, as flats) and on bias frames (read noise only) all the means agree.

It is strongly recommended correct setting of FITS_KEY_EXPTIME (environment variables) because the exposure time will usable for later processing. Chip temperatures are checked to be equal during processing when FITS_KEY_TEMPERATURE is valuable.

Input and output

On input, list of dark frame frames is expected. Optionaly, a bias can by pre-applied on every frame.

On output, a single file representing of mean dark frame is created. The file has STDERR extension with standard errors: the error of the mean by the scatter of the used values of the pixel, √(Σ(x − 〈x〉)²/(n − 1)/n), equal to the scatter of masters over realisations within 0.1% (rms) on the frames above (the error of the subtracted bias frame, common to all of them, is not in it; the robust mean of --hampel has its own error). With -bias, the keyword DARKBIAS records the bias frame; phcorr with the same bias frame then propagates the error of the bias by (1 − t), t the dark time factor. A single dark frame is bias-subtracted too (it was written as it is, while DARKBIAS said otherwise). DARKMEAN records the mean (CLIP, ARITH, HAMPEL). Masked values (if parameter -bitmask is presented), and the pixels with less than two values within the threshold and the saturation, are set to the frame mean (the clipped mean of the pixels by their scatter; the robust mean with --hampel).

Parameters

-bias file
use the specified bias frame
--arith
the arithmetic mean of the values of a pixel (no rejection of outliers)
--hampel
the robust mean of the previous versions (the Hampel M-estimator of OakLeaf), biased low on a dark of a few electrons per pixel
-B bitpix
set numerical type of output images, see Common options (default -32)
-o filename
save to the output file, see Common options

Also see Common options. A temperature and exposure time parameteres are set via Environment variables.

Examples

$ munipack dark -o d120.fits d120_*.fits
