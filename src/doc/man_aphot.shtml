Aperture Photometry

Aperture photometry determines sum of counts from a star in a set of predefined apertures.

Synopsis

munipack aphot [.. parameters ..] file(s)[,result(s)]

Running of photometry adds a HDU extensions to the original frame as a table described in Processing Format.

Algorithm

Aperture photometry is performed by the way:

  • Read parameters (command-line and gain and LOBAD,HIBAD) and list of detected stars (in FIND extension).
  • For every detected star, the sum inside of the specified apertures as well as in sky determining ring is computed.
  • The mean background in the ring is computed: the clipped mean of the pixels of the ring outside the masks of the other detected stars (see below).
  • The counts for all apertures are computed by subtracting of mean background.
  • The apertures contaminated by a brighter neighbour are invalidated (-1): the light of the neighbour in the aperture, estimated by its growth curve (its apertures, linear in the area between them: the light in the annulus d − a … d + a times the fraction of the annulus the aperture of the radius a covers at the distance d), exceeds three times the error of the aperture and three times its own error. A neighbour without valid apertures (saturated) contaminates the apertures reaching its mask. So a faint star a few pixels from a bright one, or a second detection of the same star, is not measured with the light of the bright one (a u = 17 star 6 pixels from a u = 11.4 star came out 4.4 mag too bright).

The pixels near the border are approximated by only a fraction covering the pixel inside.

By defaults, twelve apertures is used. Aperture radii are computed as points of logarithmic spiral r = r0exp a φ with the initial radius r0=2 and pitch angle 8.9073° (to be compatible with DAOPHOT II).

For a correct determination of photometry errors, the camera-specific parameters must by provided (see below).

Strictly speaking, for further processing just one aperture is required. The prepare of several apertures has profit in fast switching to another aperture without needs to do photometry again. More apertures can be also used in simultaneous use of ones for PSF of grow-curve photometry.

The results of aperture photometry are stored in columns of a newly created APERPHOT extension.

The photometry is performed for all detected stars (all stars in FIND extension) in all specified apertures. Sometimes it is impossible compute the sum, the case is indicated by a negative value (-1). Common situation are when the sum of counts with subtracted background is negative (sky is polluted by a bright source, background has a fast gradient) or the star is too near of border and summing pixels are out of area) or a star is saturated.

The results of aperture photometry are stored to next PHOTOMETRY extension. In the extension, the results in a selected aperture (by -s) are selected. Just stars with valid (non-negative) values are presented here.

The sky

The aperture sums the sky pixels, so the sky subtracted from it must be the mean level of the sky; the mode, the median or a robust mean bias the counts by the difference times the area of the aperture. A sky of a few photo-electrons per pixel (a space telescope in the ultraviolet, short exposures) has a skewed, discrete (Poisson) distribution whose robust means lie well below its mean (by 13% at 0.4 e-/pixel and the read noise 0.37 e-, by 30% at 0.1 e-/pixel). The sky of a star is therefore:

  • The pixels of the ring, except those inside the masks of the other stars of FIND. The mask of a star follows its light outwards (the mean of 1-pixel annuli above the sky of the frame by two standard errors), so faint stars get small masks, bright, saturated or haloed stars large ones (at most half of the outer ring radius). When the masks leave less than a quarter of the ring (crowded fields), the whole ring is used.
  • Their mean clipped at t − 5σ … t + q + √(q² + (5σ)²), iterated from the median, where t is the mean, σ is the larger of the scatter of the pixels and the noise model √((t − BIASLEV)/GAIN + READNS²) [ADU] (the read noise READNS [ADU] or RDNOISE [e-]; without GAIN, the scatter alone), and q = 25/6 photo-electrons (25/(6 GAIN) ADU). The upper limit is the Bernstein bound of the Poisson tail at the probability of 5σ, so no photon of the sky is clipped even when σ is less than one electron, while stars, cosmics and hot pixels are. (One routine with the sky of find and psfphot, the stack of kombine and the master of dark.)
  • SKYERR is the scatter of the used pixels divided by the square root of their number.

On simulated QUVIK-like frames (0.1–2000 e-/pixel, gain 0.1 and 1.5 e-/ADU) the median SKY agrees with the true background within 0.002 e-/pixel (0.5% at 0.1 e-/pixel), the flux of faint stars is unbiased and SKYERR matches the scatter of the sky between noise realisations. The light of undetected stars is part of the mean sky (it is in the apertures as well).

The errors

APCOUNTERR [ADU] of an aperture of the area A = Σf (f the fractions of the pixels inside the aperture) is 

  APCOUNTERR² = APCOUNT/GAIN + Σf² σ² + A² SKYERR²

the Poisson noise of the star (the counts in ADU divided by the gain [e-/ADU] of the frame), the noise of the sky pixels in the aperture (σ the scatter of the sky pixels of the ring, which includes the read noise; a partial pixel adds f² of its variance) and the error of the mean sky. The gain is GAIN of the frame (1 for frames in electrons, BUNIT = COUNTS of phcorr); without GAIN, 1 e-/ADU is assumed with a warning. The gain used is the keyword APERRGN of APERPHOT. Versions before 0.6.2+hg1653 (patched) assumed 1 e-/ADU for every frame: on a frame with the gain 0.101 e-/ADU the errors of bright stars were 2.5–3 times smaller than their scatter.

Data assumption

Aphot does assumtion, that data represents actually detected counts, or that the gain of the frame (GAIN) converts them to electrons for the errors.

While common digital devices detects single photons, the cameras does not report their true counts. Rather, they reports a digitised value which is multiplied by some amplifier gain of the electronic device. Moreover, the value has included bias to suppress a non-linearity of the devices.

Therefore, it is very important to pre-correct the data. The relative precision of star determination is driven by Poisson statistics; if actually detected counts of photons is N, the relative error will be σ=√N/N. However, data multiplied by factor g gives σ/√g (for N=10000, σ=1/100, while a choice g=4 leads to 1/50).

Aphot applies GAIN of the frame to the Poisson noise of stars (see the errors above), so the errors are right for raw frames with GAIN as well. Carefully handled parameters of phcorr will prepare data in electrons. The gain value can be checked by flat-field.

Elliptic apertures

Munipack improvement over the original DAOPHOT aperture photometry implementation is possibility to use of elliptic apertures enclosing trailed stars more tightly. While this idea looks extremely attractive, the application on real data shows minimal, if any, progress. This is perhaps a dead way.

The implementation is pure experimental. FIND estimates both eccentricity and inclination; the values are used by this routine, if it is invoked with --enable-ellipticity. Elliptic apertures are missing intentionally in GUI.

Aperture Corrections: the Enclosed Energy

An aperture contains only a fraction of the light of a star, its enclosed energy EE (the fraction of the total flux). When the PSF changes over the field, so does EE, and the aperture photometry of stars at different positions are on different scales. Optionally, aphot records EE of every star in every aperture: the columns APEEi and APEEERRi (its error) of APERPHOT (format). APCOUNTi/APEEi is the total flux of the star; phcal calibrates it when APEE is present. Two sources:

A known PSF (--psf-file, or --psf GAUSS|MOFFAT)
The PSF file of artificial and psfphot (format; the same field-position convention as psfphot: pixel offsets from NAXIS/2 or --psf-centre, divided by the WCS scale or --scale), or an analytic Gauss or Moffat PSF of --fwhm (the FWHM of FIND by default) and --beta. The PSF at the position of the star is pixelised at its sub-pixel position and summed with exactly the partial-pixel weights of the apertures, so APCOUNT/APEE is the total flux of the model (on noiseless frames rendered with the PSF file, APCOUNT/APEE equals the true flux to 1e-6 in every aperture). APEEERR is zero.
The frame's stars (--apcorr auto)
The apertures of a star are its growth curve. The bright (S/N > 30 in the aperture of 2 FWHM, at least 30 stars), unsaturated stars with all the apertures valid and without a comparable neighbour (brighter than 1%) within 5.5 FWHM give EE relative to their total flux T: the mean of three apertures of the plateau of the growth curves. A growth curve is C(r) = T EE(r) + b A(r), A the area and b a residual sky per pixel (the error of the sky of the star, the light of fainter neighbours inside the apertures, which the sky ring masks); on the plateau EE = 1 and C = T + b A. The plateau is the first window of three consecutive apertures, from the first one larger than 1.3 FWHM, whose slope relative to T (fitted for every star, the errors by the covariance of the nested apertures, averaged over the stars with their excess scatter, clipped at 3σ) is consistent with zero within 2σ, so the light of the PSF wing is not left out of T. The search ends at twice the radius enclosing 99% of the stacked growth curve (the median of the curves normalised by the aperture of 2 FWHM, relative to its level: the median of the three apertures from 4 FWHM, or its maximum; for a Gaussian PSF 6.1σ, 2.6 FWHM); when no window passes up to there, the plateau starts at the last aperture within the cap (EEPLAT = CAP). The cap keeps the total flux off the large apertures, which hold the light of the fainter neighbours, more on dense fields: before, the first aperture within 0.5% of the level of the stacked curve was taken, and on dense frames the plateau went out to 20–40 pixels and T was 0.1–0.4% too large. The fitted b of the plateau (EEPLATB, ADU/pixel, its error EEPLATBE), consistent with zero, is recorded and not removed (that would add its error times the area to T). Use apertures out to the sky ring, for example --apertures ...,20,25,30,35,40 --ring 45,60; the median slope of the counts by the area over the apertures larger than 4 FWHM (three at least) is recorded as a diagnostic of the sky (EESKYBIA, ADU/pixel; zero within its noise for the mean sky, it includes the light of extended PSF wings and of neighbours); it is not removed. EE of every aperture is fitted by a polynomial over the field of the order --apcorr-var 0, 1, 2 (default 2) in x,y, or in the distance from the field centre with --apcorr-radial (for optics whose PSF changes with the field radius); the fit is weighted, robust (3σ clipping, starting from the median), with the excess scatter of stars over their errors added. The error of EEk = Ck/T of a star takes the nesting of the apertures into account (they share the pixels and the sky): Var = [V(Ck) − 2 EEk Cov(Ck,T) + EEk² V(T)]/T², Cov(Ci,Cj) = Vp(Cmin(i,j)) + AiAj SKYERR², where Vp = APCOUNTERR² − A² SKYERR² is the noise of the pixels of the smaller aperture and A the areas (taken as independent, the errors were 4–15 times too large and no variation could be significant). The order is the constant, raised while the higher terms decrease χ² by the weights of the lower order (with its excess scatter) at the 99.9% level, at most --apcorr-var and three stars per parameter (with a warning for fewer stars), so a constant PSF gives a constant EE and the scatter of EE of single stars (neighbours, sub-pixel positions) is not taken for a variation; the model is not extrapolated beyond the stars. APEEERR is the error of the polynomial at the star and the excess scatter. The header records the stars, the orders, the plateau (EERTOT1, EERTOT2, EEPLAT, EEPLATZ, EEPLATB, EER99, EERCAP), the sky bias and the scatter.

Without these options, aphot writes no APEE columns (as before). --coo does not support them.

Input And Output

On input, list of frames containing the table with already detected stars is expected.

On output, tables with the aperture photometry and results from the selected column, represented by FITS extension, is added to all input images.

Parameters

--apertures r1, r2, .. rmax
define apertures radii (in pixels), count and sizes are arbitrary, must be specified in increasing order and separated by commas
--ring ri,ro
inner and outer sky ring radius in pixels, the inner radius must be equal or bigger than the greater aperture
--enable-ellipticity
Apertures are ellipses; their actual shape is determined by FITS parameter in FIND extension, or specified by following command line options.
--eccentricity e
Eccentricity of apertures by a contour ellipse: 0 ≤ e < 1. The default value is e=0 meaning circular aperture.
--inclination i
Inclination of major semi-axis of the ellipse in degrees. Positive values of the angle are in mathematics sense (counter-clockwise).
--psf-file file
the enclosed energy (APEE) by the PSF file (see above)
--psf GAUSS|MOFFAT|FILE
the enclosed energy by an analytic PSF (FILE requires --psf-file)
--fwhm pix, --beta β
FWHM and the Moffat exponent of the analytic PSF (defaults: FWHM of FIND, 2.5)
--scale pix/deg, --psf-centre x,y
the scale (default: WCS of the frame) and the field centre (default: NAXIS/2) of the PSF file, as in psfphot; the field centre also for --apcorr-radial
--apcorr auto
the enclosed energy by the growth curves of the frame's stars
--apcorr-var 0|1|2
the order of its variation over the field (default: 2)
--apcorr-radial
the variation by the distance from the field centre (default: x,y)
--coo "x1,y1 x2,y2..."
List of pixel rectangular coordinates: they are centroids of stars to get photometry for. Their photometry processing, including accuracy and robustness, is exactly the same as the canonical way. FIND extension can not be presented in the FITS file.

See Common options for input/output filenames.

History

The routine is on base of original code of DAOPHOT II by P.B.Stetson (has been forked from Midas's implementation at 1996). Some additional enhancements has been done:

  • Robust estimators replaces the original estimators of averadge. The sky is the clipped mean by the noise model, the detected stars masked (a robust mean of the ring before 2026).
  • All processing is done in memory (no disc buffering is used).
  • The magnitude output has been abandoned.
  • Elliptic apertures can be choosed.
  • FITS files for both input and output are used
  • Log format is changed.
  • Code is updated for Fortran 90 and 95 (and perhaps above).

Examples

Run aperture photometry:

$ munipack find pleiades.fits $ munipack aphot pleiades.fits

Select apertures and sky estimation by hand (as powers of 2):

$ munipack aphot --apertures 1,2,4,8,16,32 --ring 16,32 pleiades.fits

The enclosed energy by the field-dependent PSF file, or by the frame's stars with a radial variation (phcal then calibrates the total flux):

$ munipack aphot --psf-file psf.fits frame.fits
$ munipack aphot --apertures 1,2,3,4,5,7,9,12,15,20,25,30,35,40 --ring 45,60 \
  --apcorr auto --apcorr-radial frame.fits
$ munipack phcal --saper 5 -c cat.fits ... frame.fits

List of total counts (aperture photometry) in default apertures for star at x = 666, y = 666:

$ munipack aphot --coo "666,666" pleiades.fits
