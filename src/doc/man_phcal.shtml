Photometry Calibration

A photometry calibration.

Synopsis

munipack phcal [.. parameters ..] file(s)[,result(s)]

Description

Purpose of this routine is the photometry calibration of CCD frames. The calibration establish an exact relation between observed instrumental counts and expected amount of photons by using a (small) set of known calibration stars. On base of the calibration, all known objects and the image itself should be transformed from instrumental counts to aboslute fluxes of photons, energy or magnitudes.

Detailed Description

Every single picture elements exposed by light for a time period is collecting electrons created due to photo-electric effect from photons. Counts of the electrons is digitised and stored in output images. One can be directly used for additional light analysis. We will use term (instrumental) count(s) for their calculated amount (widely used by astronomers).

For a non-ideal detector, amount of digitised electrons will less than for photons. These counts will depends on the instrumental equipment (quality of optical path, lenses, mirrors, weather, quantum efficiency of CCD camera, etc). As consequence of the measurement process, the counts is unique per an apparatus and observation conditions. Two telescopes equipped with the same instrumentation will not product the same counts due to atmospheric conditions. The different apparatus (filters, detectors) generally produces different counts. To compare and to process observations from different observatories, we need unify all observations to same scales. Traditionally, in various parts of physics, the calibration is done with setting of calibration scale on a priory known calibrated sources. The sources are calibration stars for astronomers. The scale is relation between observed counts and produced photons.

More detailed description of the calibration can be found in Photometry Overview.

The calibration implemented by Munipack follows these steps:

  • Detected stars are merged using of coordinates against to a reference photometric catalogue.
  • With known filter and photometry system, expected count of photons is determined from magnitudes in the catalogue.
  • Instrumental counts are transformed from an instrumental to a standard (photometry system) filter using of provided transformation matrix. (This step is optional.)
  • By using of a telescope area, gain and exposure time are observed counts normalised on count rates.
  • The calibration itself is determined as the ratio of reference amount of photons and observed rates.

Image data values are converted on a physical quantity (like photons, fluxes, magnitudes). The conversion is applied on frame data as well as on photometric tables. The conversion requires known astrometry and photometry calibration.

The conversion is very useful for converting observed quantities to a derived ones. One is ideal for construction of multi-spectral pictures of objects.

Algorithm

The calibration is computed by the way:

  • Reference catalogue and frames are merged in spherical coordinates and by search in close neighbourhood.
  • From known filter and photometry system, from catalogue magnitudes photon fluxes are computed.
  • Observed counts are normalised to rates by knowing of telescope area and exposure time. The counts in an aperture are divided by its enclosed energy (APEE, when aphot provides it), so the total flux is calibrated (--apcorr).
  • The calibration ratio between expected photons and observed counts is determined by a robust method.
  • The systematic error: the scatter of the calibration stars in excess of their errors (--syserr), determined together with the ratio (see below), added in quadrature to the errors of all objects.
  • All values in frames and tables are recomputed on photons.

Systematic Error

With --syserr auto (default), the calibration ratio t (CTPH) and the systematic error σ (PHSYSERR) are determined together by the likelihood of a mixture (Hogg, Bovy & Lang 2010, arXiv:1008.4686). The logarithm of the ratio of the expected photons and the counts of a star, yi = ln(phi/cti), with the relative error ei (of both, in quadrature) is either a calibration star, distributed as N(ln t, ei² + σ²), with the probability 1 − f, or an outlier (a misidentified star, a blend, a variable, a saturated star) of a broad distribution N(ln t, ei² + σ² + B²) with the probability f. The parameters are found by the EM algorithm: from the median and σ by the median absolute deviation, the probabilities qi of the stars to be calibration stars, ln t as the mean weighted by qi/(ei² + σ²), σ by the restricted (REML) likelihood of the calibration stars, which accounts for the fitted ratio (the leverage of every star), so σ is not biased by few stars, f as the mean of 1 − qi (at most ½), and B² as the excess variance of the outliers (at least 9 (median ei² + σ²) and (0.1 mag)²). Outliers have a negligible weight in both t and σ and no star is clipped, so the stars in the tails of the distribution keep their weight. CTPHERR is the error of the weighted mean. With --verbose, σ, its interval, the number of stars, f and t are printed.

Few stars. The fraction and the width of the outliers cannot be estimated from a few stars, and their likelihood has two modes: a star deviating from the others is either an outlier or a part of the systematic scatter. EM from the robust start takes the first, so with 3–7 stars σ was 15–25% low. Below 8 stars (8 + the number of the colour coefficients with --color-term), f is fixed at 0.1 and B at 1 mag, EM runs from the robust start and from all stars as calibration stars (the plain restricted likelihood), and the mode of the larger posterior is taken: the restricted likelihood of the mixture with a weak prior of σ (half-normal, 0.1 mag: a systematic scatter of 0.2 mag is unlikely, an outlier of 0.4 mag is not). σ² is then unbiased (the mean of σ is still low by the square root of a few degrees of freedom, as for any estimate), stars far from the others are still found as outliers, while a star a few σ off cannot be told from the scatter of 3–7 stars. With 8 and more stars the results are those of the adaptive mixture (bit-identical to the previous version).

The uncertainty of σ. With few stars any σ is very uncertain (its square has the relative error √(2/(N − 1)), ±70% for 5 stars). The 68% interval is given by the profile of the restricted likelihood of the calibration stars (the values of σ where it decreases by ½ from its maximum); for σ = 0 its upper end is an upper limit. The header gets PHSYSERE, the upper error of PHSYSERR (the upper limit when PHSYSERR = 0), PHSYSNST, the number of the stars, and the interval as a COMMENT line. With fewer than 10 stars a warning is printed and written to the header.

Sparse fields. When the frames have few calibration stars (QUVIK UV frames: 2–8 stars of SDSS u on 512²–1024² frames), PHSYSERR of a single frame is little more than a guess. Determine the systematic error from many frames of the same instrument and setup (for example the mean of PHSYSERR² over the frames, each estimate of σ² being unbiased, or the frames calibrated together) and pass the value (--syserr 0.02): it is applied as given and the outliers are treated by the robust ratio.

Monte Carlo (stars with errors 0.005–0.08 mag, 2000 sets per case): with 30 and 100 stars, σ = 0.01, 0.03, 0.06 mag is recovered within 1–6% with up to 10% of outliers at 0.3–3 mag; with 10 stars the mean estimate is 7–18% low, the root mean square within 5% (the square root of an unbiased variance is low). Without outliers, σ = 0.06 mag, the mean and the root mean square of the estimate are 0.048 and 0.060 mag for 3 stars, 0.052 and 0.059 for 5, 0.055 and 0.059 for 7 stars (before the treatment of few stars 0.038 and 0.054, 0.045 and 0.054, 0.050 and 0.057); without a systematic error the mean is 1–7 mmag (the median 0). The 68% interval contains the true σ in 64–72% of the sets for 3–7 stars and σ = 0.03–0.06 mag, in 59–70% for 10–100 stars, and in 86–91% for σ = 0 (an upper limit). Outliers do not move the ratio: with 10 stars and 5% of outliers the scatter of the zero point is 3.6 mmag and σ 0.002 mag for a true 0 (the previous estimator, a robust ratio followed by the excess scatter of the residuals clipped at 3σ: 63 mmag and 0.007 mag), with 10% of outliers and σ = 0.01 mag 6 mmag and 0.008 mag (179 mmag, 0.053 mag); with 5 stars and 5% of outliers, σ = 0.01 mag, 28 mmag (a set with an outlier of a few σ among 5 stars cannot be corrected). CTPHERR does not include the uncertainty of σ: with 3–7 stars and σ ≥ 0.03 mag it contains the true zero point in 54–63% of the sets (68% expected).

Changed defaults. The systematic error is estimated by default (--syserr auto): MAGERR (and the errors of other quantities, CTPHERR) of frames whose calibration stars scatter more than their errors say are larger than in older versions, and CTPH may change within its error (the weights of the stars change). On frames whose stars scatter as their errors say, the estimate is zero or a few mmag and the results do not change. --syserr 0 gives the results of older versions. The enclosed energy is used by default when APERPHOT contains it (--apcorr none switches it off); frames processed without it give the same results as before.

Colour Term

The passband of an instrument is never that of the catalogue: QUVIK's 260–360 nm band is calibrated against SDSS u or GALEX NUV, a CCD with a V filter against Johnson V or Gaia G. The ratio of the expected photons and the counts then depends on the colour of the star, and even a calibration in one filter needs a colour term. With --color-term A,B (two catalogue columns, the colour c = A − B; A,B,errA,errB adds the columns of their errors) the calibration is

mcat − minst = ZP + k1 (c − cref) [+ k2 (c − cref)² with --color-order 2],

fitted as ln(ph/ct) = ln CTPH + b1 d + b2 d², d = c − cref, k = −2.5 b/ln 10, by the same mixture likelihood as the systematic error: the EM algorithm from a robust start (the median of the slopes of pairs of stars), the weighted least squares of the polynomial with the weights of the calibration stars, σ by the restricted likelihood with the leverage of the polynomial, so outliers have no weight in the zero point, in k nor in σ. The variance of a star includes the error of its colour propagated by the slope, (dg/dc)² σc², and its covariance with the catalogue magnitude of the calibration when the band is one of A, B (the colour B − V has the error of V). A fixed --syserr fixes σ. With few stars (above; below 9 stars for a linear term, 10 for a quadratic one) the outlier model is fixed and the mode of the likelihood chosen in the same way. The stars without a colour in the catalogue are not used.

The colour term multiplies the photons, ph = CTPH exp(g(c)) ct, so all quantities (MAG, FLUX, PHOTON, …) have it consistently. CTPH is the calibration of a star of the reference colour cref (COLTREF): by default (--color-ref mean) the weighted mean colour of the calibration stars, where CTPH is nearly uncorrelated with k1 and close to the calibration without the colour term; the calibrated image is that of a star of this colour. CTPH of different fields is comparable only for a fixed reference (--color-ref 0, the usual zero point of the zero colour, has a larger CTPHERR far from the colours of the stars). Every star of the output is matched to the catalogue (the search radius of the calibration): with a colour it gets its correction, the error of the calibration at its colour (the covariance of CTPH and the coefficients) and the error of its colour; a star without a colour (not in the catalogue, or a magnitude missing) gets the mean correction of the calibration stars and its scatter as an error, so its magnitude is right on average and its error says how uncertain it is (COLORSRC 0; kσc = 0.06 mag for k = 0.1 and the colours of a field). The table gets the columns COLOR, COLORERR, COLORSRC and the header the coefficients (format). With several filters every filter has its own colour term by the same colour. --color-term needs a catalogue (-c) and excludes --tratab (whose transformation has the colours).

Validation: a simulated V frame (400 stars, B − V from −0.2 to 1.8) calibrated against a catalogue band X = V + k(B − V) + N(0, 0.01) with catalogue B, V of errors 0.01: k = 0.0005 ± 0.0009, 0.1007 ± 0.0009, 0.2996 ± 0.0009 for k = 0, 0.1, 0.3, the slope of the residuals against the colour below 0.0008 (without the colour term −0.100, −0.300 and a systematic error of 0.058, 0.171 mag); 30 realisations of the catalogue with 5% outliers: k 0.29998, standard deviation 0.00091 for the quoted error 0.00100; the outliers (found as 5.1%) do not move k; the residual scatter is 0.0009 mag for every k with noiseless colours, as without a mismatch (the noise of the catalogue colours adds k·0.014 mag, included in MAGERR: pull 1.01); 10% of the stars without a colour: their residuals scatter by 0.058 (k = 0.1) for MAGERR 0.057. Without --color-term the results are bit-identical to the previous version.

When photometry transformation table from instrumental to standard system is available --tratab (made by phfotran), the calibration follows its model. A flux table (TRAMODEL = 'FLUX', and the tables without the keyword): the expected photons of the calibration stars in the standard filters are transformed to the counts of the instrumental filters by its forward matrix and the calibration is performed on the kind of counts (CTPH is then a correction near 1); the counts of all stars are converted to the photons of the standard filters by the inverse of the forward matrix (the backward matrix of the table, fitted independently, is not used). A magnitude table (TRAMODEL = 'MAG'): CTPH of every filter is fitted with the colour terms of the table fixed, by the catalogue colours of the calibration stars, and the magnitudes of every star are those of the standard system, m = m0 + k1 (c − cref) + k2 (c − cref)² with m0 calibrated by CTPH and c the colour of the standard magnitudes, solved for all filters together (Newton's method); MAGERR includes the errors of the coefficients, COLOR the colours. The filters of the table must be those of the calibration and its instrumental system (PHOTSYS1) that of --photsys-instr, or of the frames (PHOTSYS) without it.

Several filters. The expected photons of every filter are derived from the catalogue magnitude in the filter itself (the same conversion as artificial uses to render a frame). Without --tratab the instrumental and standard filters are identical (the transformation is the identity) and every filter is calibrated by itself: the results equal those of the frames calibrated one at a time, except that the calibration stars must be found on all the frames and the output tables contain the stars found on all of them. Colour terms of an instrument (a passband different from the standard one, the colour dependence of the extinction) are fitted with the calibration by --color-term, or described by the transformation table of phfotran; with it, the calibration and the conversion of the counts use the colours of the stars. Older versions corrected the photons of the outputs of more filters by an "effective extinction" estimated from the calibration ratios (CTPH, the efficiency of the instrument rather than of the atmosphere), not the calibration itself: the magnitudes had a colour term against the catalogue, 0.1–0.2 mag per magnitude of B−V in B on simulated frames (header keywords EXTIN_K0, EXTIN_R, EXTINREF, no longer written).

Operational modes

Photometry calibration is a very complex task so various ways are implemented:

Reference Catalogue
Reference sources are stars provided as a table. The table can be prepared by hand or a photometrical catalogue can be used. Precision of calibration is given by quality of the catalogue and also by true colour transformation of instrumental to the catalogue. Usually, the calibration has small systematical offsets but larger random errors.
Reference Frame
Reference stars are pulled from previsouly calibrated frame. Relative precision is generally better than in previous case because no colour transformation is required.
Manual Calibration
The calibration values are provided by an user.

The choice suitable for specific situation would be very difficoult.

Results

Output calibrated fits frames contains a new extension described in Photometry Calibrated File. Its table contains coordinates of stars on frames and various photometric quantities.

See Common options for input/output filenames. If advanced parameters -O are not set, default -O --mask '!\1_XXX.\2' is used according to some selected quantity. For example, -q MAG and the input file blazar_01R.fits will produce the output file blazar_01R_MAG.fits. Be warned, that output files are overwritten in any case by default.

Filters Selection

The filters of a multi-filter calibration are given by -f, the catalogue columns by --col-mag and --col-magerr, and the calibrations of the manual mode by -C, all in the same order: the n-th column (or value) belongs to the n-th filter. The frames are paired with the filters by the filter of the frame (the keyword FILTER), so neither the order of -f nor that of the files matters; the filter of every frame must be in -f. Internally, the frames are arranged by the order of the filters in the photometry system.

For example, bands in reference table are labelled as Bmag, Vmag and filters as B,V; the two commands give identical results: 

$ munipack phcal ... -f B,V --col-mag Bmag,Vmag frame_B.fits frame_V.fits
$ munipack phcal ... -f V,B --col-mag Vmag,Bmag frame_V.fits frame_B.fits

Older versions paired the catalogue columns (in the order of -f) with the frames arranged by the photometry system: with -f not in the order of the system (V,B for Johnson) the calibration failed (the second filter undefined) or a frame got the calibration of another filter.

Preparation Of Photometry Catalogue

The table is a fits table and must contains columns with Right Ascension, Declination, reference magnitudes (and optionally with standard errors of the magnitudes). The column naming is by default RA, DEC, MAG and can be changed with --col-* options.

The table may be prepared by any standard FITS utility (for example fcreate utility of FTOOLS).

To save the time, you can just edit file mtable.lst in Munipack distribution (carefully handle with NAXIS1 and NAXIS2, etc.) and create a table 

$ munipack fits --restore mtable.lst

The output in mtable.fits can be used to a right frame as 

$ munipack phcal -c mtable.fits frame.fits

A Low-Precision Calibration

We are using just one filter. No transformation matrix is used.

A High-Precision Calibration

We are using multiple filters. Transformation matrix is used, we get the maximum possible precision.

Examples

Calibrate against to UCAC5 catalogue: 

$ munipack cone -c UCAC5 -o 0716cat.fits -r 0.1 110.47 71.34
$ munipack phcal -c 0716cat.fits --col-ra RAJ2000 --col-dec DEJ2000 \
  --col-mag Gmag  --photosys Johnson 0716_?R.fits
$ munipack phcal -c T_Phe.fits --tratab phfotran.fits --photsys-ref Johnson \
   -f B,V,R,I --col-mag B,V,R,I \
   TPhe_B.fits,b.fits TPhe_V.fits,v.fits TPhe_R.fits,r.fits TPhe_I.fits,i.fits

munipack phcal --verbose -r grb140423-2_C_0002_cal.fits --photsys-ref Johnson  grb140423-2_C_0003.fits

A colour term against the catalogue colour B − V (with its errors), the instrumental band calibrated by the catalogue band of V: 

$ munipack phcal -c cat.fits --photsys-ref Johnson -f V --col-mag Vmag --col-magerr e_Vmag \
   --color-term Bmag,Vmag,e_Bmag,e_Vmag --verbose frame.fits
