Photometry Calibration

A photometry calibration.

Synopsis

munipack phcal [.. parameters ..] file(s)[,result(s)]

Description

Purpose of this routine is the photometry calibration of CCD frames. The calibration establish an exact relation between observed instrumental counts and expected amount of photons by using a (small) set of known calibration stars. On base of the calibration, all known objects and the image itself should be transformed from instrumental counts to aboslute fluxes of photons, energy or magnitudes.

Detailed Description

Every single picture elements exposed by light for a time period is collecting electrons created due to photo-electric effect from photons. Counts of the electrons is digitised and stored in output images. One can be directly used for additional light analysis. We will use term (instrumental) count(s) for their calculated amount (widely used by astronomers).

For a non-ideal detector, amount of digitised electrons will less than for photons. These counts will depends on the instrumental equipment (quality of optical path, lenses, mirrors, weather, quantum efficiency of CCD camera, etc). As consequence of the measurement process, the counts is unique per an apparatus and observation conditions. Two telescopes equipped with the same instrumentation will not product the same counts due to atmospheric conditions. The different apparatus (filters, detectors) generally produces different counts. To compare and to process observations from different observatories, we need unify all observations to same scales. Traditionally, in various parts of physics, the calibration is done with setting of calibration scale on a priory known calibrated sources. The sources are calibration stars for astronomers. The scale is relation between observed counts and produced photons.

More detailed description of the calibration can be found in Photometry Overview.

The calibration implemented by Munipack follows these steps:

  • Detected stars are merged using of coordinates against to a reference photometric catalogue.
  • With known filter and photometry system, expected count of photons is determined from magnitudes in the catalogue.
  • Instrumental counts are transformed from an instrumental to a standard (photometry system) filter using of provided transformation matrix. (This step is optional.)
  • By using of a telescope area, gain and exposure time are observed counts normalised on count rates.
  • The calibration itself is determined as the ratio of reference amount of photons and observed rates.

Image data values are converted on a physical quantity (like photons, fluxes, magnitudes). The conversion is applied on frame data as well as on photometric tables. The conversion requires known astrometry and photometry calibration.

The conversion is very useful for converting observed quantities to a derived ones. One is ideal for construction of multi-spectral pictures of objects.

Algorithm

The calibration is computed by the way:

  • Reference catalogue and frames are merged in spherical coordinates and by search in close neighbourhood.
  • From known filter and photometry system, from catalogue magnitudes photon fluxes are computed.
  • Observed counts are normalised to rates by knowing of telescope area and exposure time. The counts in an aperture are divided by its enclosed energy (APEE, when aphot provides it), so the total flux is calibrated (--apcorr).
  • The calibration ratio between expected photons and observed counts is determined by a robust method.
  • The systematic error: the scatter of the calibration stars in excess of their errors (--syserr), determined together with the ratio (see below), added in quadrature to the errors of all objects.
  • All values in frames and tables are recomputed on photons.

Systematic Error

With --syserr auto (default), the calibration ratio t (CTPH) and the systematic error σ (PHSYSERR) are determined together by the likelihood of a mixture (Hogg, Bovy & Lang 2010, arXiv:1008.4686). The logarithm of the ratio of the expected photons and the counts of a star, yi = ln(phi/cti), with the relative error ei (of both, in quadrature) is either a calibration star, distributed as N(ln t, ei² + σ²), with the probability 1 − f, or an outlier (a misidentified star, a blend, a variable, a saturated star) of a broad distribution N(ln t, ei² + σ² + B²) with the probability f. The parameters are found by the EM algorithm: from the median and σ by the median absolute deviation, the probabilities qi of the stars to be calibration stars, ln t as the mean weighted by qi/(ei² + σ²), σ by the restricted (REML) likelihood of the calibration stars, which accounts for the fitted ratio (the leverage of every star), so σ is not biased by few stars, f as the mean of 1 − qi (at most ½), and B² as the excess variance of the outliers (at least 9 (median ei² + σ²) and (0.1 mag)²). Outliers have a negligible weight in both t and σ and no star is clipped, so the stars in the tails of the distribution keep their weight. CTPHERR is the error of the weighted mean with the uncertainty of σ (below). With --verbose, σ, its interval, the number of stars, f and t are printed.

Few stars. The fraction and the width of the outliers cannot be estimated from a few stars, and their likelihood has two modes: a star deviating from the others is either an outlier or a part of the systematic scatter. EM from the robust start takes the first, so with 3–7 stars σ was 15–25% low, with 8–15 stars 7–13%. Below 16 stars (16 + the number of the colour coefficients with --color-term), f is fixed at 0.1 and B at 1 mag, EM runs from the robust start and from all stars as calibration stars (the plain restricted likelihood), and the mode of the larger posterior is taken: the restricted likelihood of the mixture with a weak prior of σ (half-normal, 0.1 mag: a systematic scatter of 0.2 mag is unlikely, an outlier of 0.4 mag is not). σ² is then unbiased (the mean of σ is still low by the square root of a few degrees of freedom, as for any estimate), stars far from the others are still found as outliers, while a star a few σ off cannot be told from the scatter of 3–7 stars. With 16 and more stars the results are those of the adaptive mixture. One degree of freedom is enough for σ (3 stars and a linear colour term), the prior keeps it finite.

The uncertainty of σ. With few stars any σ is very uncertain (its square has the relative error √(2/(N − 1)), ±70% for 5 stars). The 68% interval is given by the profile of the restricted likelihood of the calibration stars (the values of σ where it decreases by ½ from its maximum); for σ = 0 its upper end is an upper limit. The header gets PHSYSERE, the upper error of PHSYSERR (the upper limit when PHSYSERR = 0), PHSYSNST, the number of the stars, and the interval as a COMMENT line. With fewer than 10 stars a warning is printed and written to the header.

Sparse fields. When the frames have few calibration stars (QUVIK UV frames: 2–8 stars of SDSS u on 512²–1024² frames), PHSYSERR of a single frame is little more than a guess. Determine the systematic error from many frames of the same instrument and setup (for example the mean of PHSYSERR² over the frames, each estimate of σ² being unbiased, or the frames calibrated together) and pass the value (--syserr 0.02): it is applied as given and the outliers are treated by the robust ratio.

Monte Carlo (stars with errors 0.005–0.08 mag, 2000 sets per case): with 30 and 100 stars, σ = 0.01, 0.03, 0.06 mag is recovered within 1–6% with up to 10% of outliers at 0.3–3 mag; with 10 stars the mean estimate was 7–18% low (the adaptive mixture; now the mixture of few stars, below), the rms within 5% (the square root of an unbiased variance is low). Without outliers, σ = 0.06 mag, the mean and the root mean square of the estimate are 0.048 and 0.060 mag for 3 stars, 0.052 and 0.059 for 5, 0.055 and 0.059 for 7 stars (before the treatment of few stars 0.038 and 0.054, 0.045 and 0.054, 0.050 and 0.057); without a systematic error the mean is 1–7 mmag (the median 0). The 68% interval contains the true σ in 64–72% of the sets for 3–7 stars and σ = 0.03–0.06 mag, in 59–70% for 10–100 stars, and in 86–91% for σ = 0 (an upper limit). Outliers do not move the ratio: with 10 stars and 5% of outliers the scatter of the zero point is 3.6 mmag and σ 0.002 mag for a true 0 (the previous estimator, a robust ratio followed by the excess scatter of the residuals clipped at 3σ: 63 mmag and 0.007 mag), with 10% of outliers and σ = 0.01 mag 6 mmag and 0.008 mag (179 mmag, 0.053 mag); with 5 stars and 5% of outliers, σ = 0.01 mag, 28 mmag (a set with an outlier of a few σ among 5 stars cannot be corrected). With 10 stars the mixture of few stars gives 0.028 and 0.055 mag for σ = 0.03 and 0.06 (the adaptive one 0.026 and 0.053), the interval covers in 64–65% (59–61%).

The errors of the calibration. The error of the weighted mean takes σ as known, but few stars determine it poorly (from 3 stars σ = 0 is as likely as twice the true value), so it is too small exactly when σ dominates: CTPHERR contained the true zero point in 54–63% of the sets of 3–10 stars with σ ≥ 0.03 mag (68% expected). With σ estimated, the covariance of the calibration (CTPHERR, the colour terms COLTK1E, COLTK2E, and by them the part of MAGERR from the calibration) is that of Knapp & Hartung (2003, Stat. Med. 22, 2693; the random-effects meta-regression, the same model of values with known errors and an excess variance) in the modification of Röver, Knapp & Friede (2015, BMC Med. Res. Methodol. 15, 99):

C = t² max(1, Q) (X′UX)−1, Q = Σ ui ri² / ν,

ui = qi/(ei² + σ²), ri the residuals, ν = Σ qi − p the degrees of freedom of the calibration stars (p the coefficients) and t the 68.27% quantile of Student's t of ν degrees of freedom (1.84 for ν = 1, 1.32 for 2, 1.14 for 4, 1.05 for 10, 1.02 for 30). Q is the scatter of the calibration stars in the units of their errors with σ: when σ is underestimated (often zero from few stars) the stars scatter more and Q > 1; the floor keeps the errors at least those of the weighted mean; t is the distribution of a mean divided by an error estimated from ν degrees of freedom. Monte Carlo of the calibration (as above, no outliers), the coverage of CTPHERR for σ = 0.01, 0.03, 0.06 mag: 3 stars 0.76, 0.67, 0.65 (0.63, 0.55, 0.54 before), 5 stars 0.70, 0.65, 0.66 (0.62, 0.58, 0.60), 7 stars 0.69, 0.68, 0.67 (0.64, 0.62, 0.63), 10 stars 0.68, 0.67, 0.66 (0.62, 0.60, 0.59), 30 and 100 stars 0.65–0.68 (0.65–0.69); of k1 of a linear colour term 0.66–0.76 for 5–100 stars (0.59–0.68 before), 0.73–0.90 for 3 stars (0.26–0.61); with 5% of outliers the same within 0.03. For σ = 0 the errors are conservative, 0.70–0.87 (0.96 for 3 stars and a colour term): few stars cannot tell σ = 0 from 0.03 mag, and the intervals that cover 0.03 mag cover more at 0. Marginalising over the posterior of σ (the restricted likelihood with the prior of σ) covers 68% for σ ≥ 0.03 as well, but 85–96% for σ = 0 with errors 1.3–1.8 times larger. With 30 and more stars the errors grow by 1–6% (100 stars: 1–3%). A fixed --syserr keeps the errors of the weighted mean.

Changed defaults. The systematic error is estimated by default (--syserr auto): MAGERR (and the errors of other quantities, CTPHERR) of frames whose calibration stars scatter more than their errors say are larger than in older versions, and CTPH may change within its error (the weights of the stars change). On frames whose stars scatter as their errors say, the estimate is zero or a few mmag and the results do not change. --syserr 0 gives the results of older versions. The enclosed energy is used by default when APERPHOT contains it (--apcorr none switches it off); frames processed without it give the same results as before.

Colour Term

The passband of an instrument is never that of the catalogue: QUVIK's 260–360 nm band is calibrated against SDSS u or GALEX NUV, a CCD with a V filter against Johnson V or Gaia G. The ratio of the expected photons and the counts then depends on the colour of the star, and even a calibration in one filter needs a colour term. With --color-term A,B (two catalogue columns, the colour c = A − B; A,B,errA,errB adds the columns of their errors) the calibration is

mcat − minst = ZP + k1 (c − cref) [+ k2 (c − cref)² with --color-order 2],

fitted as ln(ph/ct) = ln CTPH + b1 d + b2 d², d = c − cref, k = −2.5 b/ln 10, by the same mixture likelihood as the systematic error: the EM algorithm from a robust start (the median of the slopes of pairs of stars), the weighted least squares of the polynomial with the weights of the calibration stars, σ by the restricted likelihood with the leverage of the polynomial, so outliers have no weight in the zero point, in k nor in σ. The variance of a star includes the error of its colour propagated by the slope, (dg/dc)² σc², and its covariance with the catalogue magnitude of the calibration when the band is one of A, B (the colour B − V has the error of V); with the error columns the fit is corrected for the errors of the colours (below). A fixed --syserr fixes σ. With few stars (above; below 17 stars for a linear term, 18 for a quadratic one) the outlier model is fixed and the mode of the likelihood chosen in the same way. The errors of the coefficients include the uncertainty of σ (above). The stars without a colour in the catalogue are not used.

The colour term multiplies the photons, ph = CTPH exp(g(c)) ct, so all quantities (MAG, FLUX, PHOTON, …) have it consistently. CTPH is the calibration of a star of the reference colour cref (COLTREF): by default (--color-ref mean) the weighted mean colour of the calibration stars, where CTPH is nearly uncorrelated with k1 and close to the calibration without the colour term; the calibrated image is that of a star of this colour. CTPH of different fields is comparable only for a fixed reference (--color-ref 0, the usual zero point of the zero colour, has a larger CTPHERR far from the colours of the stars). Every star of the output is matched to the catalogue (the search radius of the calibration): with a colour it gets its correction, the error of the calibration at its colour (the covariance of CTPH and the coefficients) and the error of its colour; a star without a colour (not in the catalogue, or a magnitude missing) gets the mean correction of the calibration stars and its scatter as an error, so its magnitude is right on average and its error says how uncertain it is (COLORSRC 0; kσc = 0.06 mag for k = 0.1 and the colours of a field). The table gets the columns COLOR, COLORERR, COLORSRC and the header the coefficients (format). With several filters every filter has its own colour term by the same colour. --color-term needs a catalogue (-c) and excludes --tratab (whose transformation has the colours).

Without --color-term the results are bit-identical to the previous version.

Errors of the catalogue colours

The colour of a star in the catalogue is its true colour plus an error, c = ξ + δ, and the fit against c instead of ξ is biased: towards zero by the reliability ratio λ = var(ξ)/var(c) (the regression dilution) and, when the colour contains the catalogue band of the calibration (u − g against u), by the error of the band, which is in both, cov/var(c):

kfit ≈ λ k + σA²/var(c) (the colour A − B against the band A).

SDSS u − g of stars fainter than u = 20.5 has the errors 0.40 mag (rms) for the spread 0.72 mag, λ = 0.70; the fit weights the stars by their errors (with the colour errors propagated), so its effective λ is 0.90–0.98, and the bias of k (by both terms) is 0.01–0.03 mag/mag, one to two errors of 100 stars. With the error columns (--color-term A,B,errA,errB) the weighted least squares is corrected for the errors of the colours (the moments less their expected parts by the errors, Fuller 1987, Measurement Error Models: the sums of w c² less w σc², of w c y less w cov(c, y); with --color-order 2 the powers by their unbiased estimates for Gaussian errors, c² − σ², c³ − 3σ²c, c⁴ − 6σ²c² + 3σ⁴, and the products with y), for one colour and for several (below; the covariances of colours sharing a band included). The errors of the coefficients are those of the corrected estimate (larger by about 1/λ). The covariance of the catalogue magnitude of the calibration and a colour containing it, −σA² (A − B against A) or +σB² (against B), is by the error column of that band in --color-term, the one of the correction of the colour; when --col-magerr gives other errors for the same column, a warning (with the magnitude error of 0.002 and the colour error of 0.3 for the same g, the covariance was left out: k −0.38 for −0.30; now −0.300). The calibration band may be either of the two (u − g against u or against g).

The assumption. The correction is right for a catalogue measured with the quoted errors: the catalogue colour is the true colour plus an independent error of the σ of the error columns (the classical model). It is not for

  • a catalogue whose colours are exact, or more precise than their errors say, e.g. a simulation made directly from the catalogue (frames rendered from the catalogue magnitudes): omit the error columns or use --color-err-correct no;
  • a simulation that draws the true magnitudes about the catalogue (the truth the catalogue plus its errors, the Berkson case: the catalogue colour is then the expected value of the true one, and the fit has no dilution): it is over-corrected, mildly for one colour (k1 −0.682 for the uncorrected −0.655 on 12 QUVIK-like frames as below) and strongly for two correlated colours (−1.03 for −0.86 on the SDSS cones, −1.13 on the frames); use --color-err-correct no;
  • errors that are not those of the measurement of the true colour (systematic errors of the catalogue, a colour transformed from another system with the scatter of the transformation as its error).

The cap. When the colours are hardly wider than their errors, the correction (1/λ) is a ratio of noisy moments with a heavy tail: in a Monte Carlo with the SDSS errors doubled and tripled (the unweighted λ below zero) the corrected k of 10–100 stars had an unbounded standard deviation (a few sets with the corrected variance near zero), and a nearly singular corrected matrix (6 stars and the order 2) gave errors of 1012. The correction is therefore capped: the corrected variance of every combination of the terms (the colour, its square, the other colours) is at least 0.5 of the observed one, the correction at most a factor of 2. A capped fit keeps a part of the dilution (biased towards zero); it is reported by a warning and a COMMENT (COLTDILU = T). Tried in the Monte Carlo: the fall-back to the uncorrected fit below the cap (discontinuous, the whole dilution left in those sets), the caps 0.3 (the tail back: the standard deviation 1.2–1.5 times that of 0.5 for 10 stars of tripled errors) and 0.7 (for 10 such stars a smaller scatter, 0.36 instead of 0.44 for k = −0.686, but it caps 5 of the 12 QUVIK-like frames of two colours below, moving them from their validated values, and up to 20% of the sets of 100 stars). With the cap 0.5 the SDSS errors as they are cap at most 3% of the sets (of k ≈ 0, whose weights do not include the colour errors; none for |k| ≥ 0.1 and 30 or more stars), and the results of several colours of 30 and more stars are unchanged.

The header. With the error columns: COLTDILU = T when the fit is corrected (F with --color-err-correct no, or when no correction is possible), COLTLAM the reliability ratio of the colour of the calibration stars (the weighted variance of the true colours over that of the catalogue colours; COLTLAMj for the colour j of several), a COMMENT; without the error columns the outputs are those of the previous version, bit for bit, and so are those with --color-err-correct no (only COLTDILU, COLTLAM and the COMMENT added).

Several colours

A passband may depend on more than one colour: QUVIK's 260–360 nm band against SDSS u follows u − g and g − r (the band extends below u, where the spectrum of a star is not a function of u − g alone), and one colour, of either order, leaves 0.02–0.03 mag about its best function (above). --color-term is repeatable, every occurrence adds a colour (at most three): 

$ munipack phcal -c sdss.fits --photsys-ref UVSIM -f q310 --col-mag upmag --col-magerr e_upmag \
   --color-term upmag,gpmag,e_upmag,e_gpmag --color-term gpmag,rpmag,e_gpmag,e_rpmag frame.fits

and the calibration is

mcat − minst = ZP + Σj k1,j (cj − cref,j) [+ k2,j (cj − cref,j)²],

--color-order and --color-ref take one value for all the colours or a list of one for every colour in the order of --color-term (--color-order 2,1, --color-ref mean,0.5); no cross terms (c1c2) are fitted: the quadratic terms of the QUVIK-like band are zero within their errors (below). The colours must be independent (u − g, g − u, or u − g, g − r, u − r are refused). The fit is that of one colour (the mixture likelihood, σ, the errors with its uncertainty) with these differences:

  • The errors of the colours of a star are correlated when they share a band: u − g and g − r have the covariance −σg² (by the error columns); the variance of a star is (∇g)′ S ∇g − 2 (∇g)′ c, S the covariance of its colours, c their covariances with the catalogue magnitude of the calibration, and the errors of the outputs (MAGERR) have the same.
  • The colours of the stars are strongly correlated (0.96 for u − g and g − r of SDSS stars), so are the coefficients (COLTC12 ≈ −0.95) and their errors are several times those of one colour; the scatter of the catalogue colours about the true ones then biases the coefficients towards zero (the regression dilution, amplified by the correlation: 4% and 10% of k = −0.3, +0.2 with SDSS errors, one to two errors of 100 stars). The weighted least squares of several colours is corrected for the errors of the colours (Errors of the catalogue colours, as for one colour; the quadratic terms too, the cap in the worst combination of the colours): the bias is below 0.15 of the errors. The correction assumes that the catalogue colours are the true ones plus their errors (a measured catalogue); a simulation that draws the true magnitudes about the catalogue (a catalogue more precise than its errors say) is over-corrected (−1.03 for −0.86): give such a catalogue without the error columns or use --color-err-correct no.
  • The calibration stars are those with all the colours. A star of the output without some of its colours (COLORSRC the bit j − 1 set for the colour j of the catalogue: 3 both of two, 2 only the second, …) gets the missing ones predicted by the known ones by the colours of the calibration stars as a Gaussian (the conditional mean, the colour–colour relation of the field) with the conditional scatter as their error, which for u − g by g − r is a fraction of the scatter of the colours; without any colour, the mean colours and their covariance. On the M67 frame below with 15% of the stars without u: their residuals scatter by 0.051 mag for MAGERR 0.056 (the mean correction of u − g instead: 0.27 mag for 0.17); without r 0.018 mag for MAGERR 0.026.
  • The table gets COLOR2, COLOR2ERR (COLOR3, COLOR3ERR) after COLORSRC, the header COLTNTRM, the keys of the colour j ≥ 2 (COLTERMj, COLTREFj, COLTjK1, COLTjK1E, COLTjK2, COLTjK2E, COLTjCOV, COLTMEAj, COLTSTDj) and the correlations COLTC12, COLTC13, COLTC23 of the linear coefficients (format); the keys of the first colour are those of one colour.

QUVIK. For a 260–360 nm band calibrated against SDSS u use u − g and g − r, linear, with the error columns. It needs enough stars: the coefficients of 15–67 calibration stars of a 2048² frame have the errors 0.10–0.42 (u − g) and 0.22–0.72 mag/mag (g − r); a frame of few stars should not fit two colours (σ and three coefficients from 2–8 stars).

When photometry transformation table from instrumental to standard system is available --tratab (made by phfotran), the calibration follows its model. A flux table (TRAMODEL = 'FLUX', and the tables without the keyword): the expected photons of the calibration stars in the standard filters are transformed to the counts of the instrumental filters by its forward matrix and the calibration is performed on the kind of counts (CTPH is then a correction near 1); the counts of all stars are converted to the photons of the standard filters by the inverse of the forward matrix (the backward matrix of the table, fitted independently, is not used). A magnitude table (TRAMODEL = 'MAG'): CTPH of every filter is fitted with the colour terms of the table fixed, by the catalogue colours of the calibration stars, and the magnitudes of every star are those of the standard system, m = m0 + k1 (c − cref) + k2 (c − cref)² with m0 calibrated by CTPH and c the colour of the standard magnitudes, solved for all filters together (Newton's method); MAGERR includes the errors of the coefficients, COLOR the colours. The filters of the table must be those of the calibration and its instrumental system (PHOTSYS1) that of --photsys-instr, or of the frames (PHOTSYS) without it.

Several filters. The expected photons of every filter are derived from the catalogue magnitude in the filter itself (the same conversion as artificial uses to render a frame). Without --tratab the instrumental and standard filters are identical (the transformation is the identity) and every filter is calibrated by itself: the results equal those of the frames calibrated one at a time, except that the calibration stars must be found on all the frames and the output tables contain the stars found on all of them. Colour terms of an instrument (a passband different from the standard one, the colour dependence of the extinction) are fitted with the calibration by --color-term, or described by the transformation table of phfotran; with it, the calibration and the conversion of the counts use the colours of the stars. Older versions corrected the photons of the outputs of more filters by an "effective extinction" estimated from the calibration ratios (CTPH, the efficiency of the instrument rather than of the atmosphere), not the calibration itself: the magnitudes had a colour term against the catalogue, 0.1–0.2 mag per magnitude of B−V in B on simulated frames (header keywords EXTIN_K0, EXTIN_R, EXTINREF, no longer written).

Operational modes

Photometry calibration is a very complex task so various ways are implemented:

Reference Catalogue
Reference sources are stars provided as a table. The table can be prepared by hand or a photometrical catalogue can be used. Precision of calibration is given by quality of the catalogue and also by true colour transformation of instrumental to the catalogue. Usually, the calibration has small systematical offsets but larger random errors.
Reference Frame
Reference stars are pulled from previsouly calibrated frame. Relative precision is generally better than in previous case because no colour transformation is required.
Manual Calibration
The calibration values are provided by an user.

The choice suitable for specific situation would be very difficoult.

Results

Output calibrated fits frames contains a new extension described in Photometry Calibrated File. Its table contains coordinates of stars on frames and various photometric quantities.

See Common options for input/output filenames. If advanced parameters -O are not set, default -O --mask '!\1_XXX.\2' is used according to some selected quantity. For example, -q MAG and the input file blazar_01R.fits will produce the output file blazar_01R_MAG.fits. Be warned, that output files are overwritten in any case by default.

Filters Selection

The filters of a multi-filter calibration are given by -f, the catalogue columns by --col-mag and --col-magerr, and the calibrations of the manual mode by -C, all in the same order: the n-th column (or value) belongs to the n-th filter. The frames are paired with the filters by the filter of the frame (the keyword FILTER), so neither the order of -f nor that of the files matters; the filter of every frame must be in -f. Internally, the frames are arranged by the order of the filters in the photometry system.

For example, bands in reference table are labelled as Bmag, Vmag and filters as B,V; the two commands give identical results: 

$ munipack phcal ... -f B,V --col-mag Bmag,Vmag frame_B.fits frame_V.fits
$ munipack phcal ... -f V,B --col-mag Vmag,Bmag frame_V.fits frame_B.fits

Older versions paired the catalogue columns (in the order of -f) with the frames arranged by the photometry system: with -f not in the order of the system (V,B for Johnson) the calibration failed (the second filter undefined) or a frame got the calibration of another filter.

Preparation Of Photometry Catalogue

The table is a fits table and must contains columns with Right Ascension, Declination, reference magnitudes (and optionally with standard errors of the magnitudes). The column naming is by default RA, DEC, MAG and can be changed with --col-* options.

The table may be prepared by any standard FITS utility (for example fcreate utility of FTOOLS).

To save the time, you can just edit file mtable.lst in Munipack distribution (carefully handle with NAXIS1 and NAXIS2, etc.) and create a table 

$ munipack fits --restore mtable.lst

The output in mtable.fits can be used to a right frame as 

$ munipack phcal -c mtable.fits frame.fits

A Low-Precision Calibration

We are using just one filter. No transformation matrix is used.

A High-Precision Calibration

We are using multiple filters. Transformation matrix is used, we get the maximum possible precision.

Examples

Calibrate against to UCAC5 catalogue: 

$ munipack cone -c UCAC5 -o 0716cat.fits -r 0.1 110.47 71.34
$ munipack phcal -c 0716cat.fits --col-ra RAJ2000 --col-dec DEJ2000 \
  --col-mag Gmag  --photosys Johnson 0716_?R.fits
$ munipack phcal -c T_Phe.fits --tratab phfotran.fits --photsys-ref Johnson \
   -f B,V,R,I --col-mag B,V,R,I \
   TPhe_B.fits,b.fits TPhe_V.fits,v.fits TPhe_R.fits,r.fits TPhe_I.fits,i.fits

munipack phcal --verbose -r grb140423-2_C_0002_cal.fits --photsys-ref Johnson  grb140423-2_C_0003.fits

A colour term against the catalogue colour B − V (with its errors, so the fit is corrected for them), the instrumental band calibrated by the catalogue band of V: 

$ munipack phcal -c cat.fits --photsys-ref Johnson -f V --col-mag Vmag --col-magerr e_Vmag \
   --color-term Bmag,Vmag,e_Bmag,e_Vmag --verbose frame.fits

The same for frames simulated with the true magnitudes drawn about the catalogue by its errors (the catalogue colour is then the mean of the true one, Errors of the catalogue colours): the errors of the colours in the variances of the stars, the fit not corrected (for frames rendered from the catalogue magnitudes themselves omit the error columns): 

$ munipack phcal -c cat.fits --photsys-ref Johnson -f V --col-mag Vmag --col-magerr e_Vmag \
   --color-term Bmag,Vmag,e_Bmag,e_Vmag --color-err-correct no frame.fits

Two colour terms, a QUVIK-like UV band against SDSS u with the colours u − g and g − r (Several colours): 

$ munipack phcal -c sdss.fits --photsys-ref UVSIM -f q310 --col-mag upmag --col-magerr e_upmag \
   --color-term upmag,gpmag,e_upmag,e_gpmag --color-term gpmag,rpmag,e_gpmag,e_rpmag frame.fits
