Find Stars

Detects stars on frames. The stars are supposed to be a Gaussian-like peaks with FWHM provided by user. Additional parameters are used to reject false detection due to various defects.

Synopsis

munipack find [.. parameters ..] file(s)[,result(s)]

Description

Fully automatic detection of stars on frames is very important and also difficult. Munipack uses an algorithm developed by P.B.Stetson for DAOPHOT II with small changes. The algorithm is very robust, insensitive to wide range of defects including hot pixels or cosmic-ray events. One also detect stars on varying background (Moon shine, in galaxies etc).

Stars are detected on base of their shapes. One supposes that second moments of stars are similar in vertical and horizontal axes with value nearly to FWHM. We also suppose that PSF of stars is not too sharp. The limits of parameters can be tuned (while it is not needed frequently): round and sharp parameters are in detail described in DAOPHOT - A computer program for crowded-field stellar photometry by P.B.Stetson.

Algorithm

Stars detection algorithm:

  • As the preparation step, the background level with the noise B,σ of image is determined on a grid covering full frame: the mean clipped by the noise model of the frame (GAIN, READNS or RDNOISE, BIASLEV), as the sky of aphot; σ is the observed scatter of the sky pixels, which includes the read noise (the read noise was added to it once more before 0.6.2+hg1653 (patched): with READNS in the header, the threshold was too high, 14 instead of 113 noise detections on a Gaussian 2048² frame at -th 4).
  • As the first step, the image is convoluted with Gaussian hat with FWHM provided by user. The convolution has effect of a digital filter which emphasizes star-like objects and suppress point defects or large-scale gradients.
  • The convoluted image is searched for local peaks. For every peak, a maximum value is determined. A star candidate has the value above the threshold (see Threshold: the height which the noise of the sky exceeds with the probability of a Normal deviate above t; parameter t corresponds to -th switch, or it is set by --false-rate) and below saturation S limit (given by --saturate)
  • The parameters round and sharp are determined for every candidate. The candidate is stored and classified as a star when both parameters lie inside acceptable intervals (tune it via --rndlo,--rndhi,--shrplo,--shrphi options), or within their noise of the values of a star (see Shape tests).
  • The wings of bright stars (a halo, a broad PSF, a saturated core) carry the noise of their light, far above the noise of the sky, and their curvature adds to the convolution: noise peaks in the wings would pass the threshold of the sky. A candidate within 8 FWHM (20 pixels at least) of a brighter candidate, or of a saturated area (farther by three times its radius), is a star only when its convolution exceeds the convolution of the brighter star's wing at the same distance (the median over the annulus ±1 pixel around the brighter star, its radial trend removed, the candidate left out) by the threshold times the noise of the wing: the noise of the sky raised by the photon noise of the wing light (the median of the annulus, with GAIN), or the scatter of the convolution over the annulus (without GAIN); the threshold is the height of the same false-alarm probability for the Poisson noise of the sky and the wing. On the sky alone this is the usual threshold. On simulated frames with a PSF of a 1.5 pixel core and an extended halo the secondary detections around bright stars dropped from 9.3 to 1.9 per frame, with the same completeness of companions at 1–3 FWHM. Any brighter candidate counts (twice brighter at least before 2026-10): a filter narrower than the stars (FWHM 2.7 for a PSF of 4.9 pixels) finds noise peaks of a star's own profile about 2 pixels from its centre with 0.6–0.9 of its height; these twin detections dropped from 133 to 78 per frame of 2401 stars.
  • Rectangular coordinates and peak ratio are determined for every star.

The data under level B-uσ (where parameter u corresponds to --lothresh) are not used during the detection process.

This routine adds a FIND extensions to original frames as a table, its is described in Find Format. The threshold and the shape tests are reported by its keywords THRESH (t), HTHRESH (the height threshold in σh), SKEWFILT and KURTFILT (the skewness and the excess kurtosis of the convolved sky by the noise model), FALSERAT (the expected number of noise peaks above the threshold per frame), SHAPESIG, SHARPREF and ROUNDREF; --verbose prints them.

Threshold

The convolution h = Σ wk dk (the lowered Gaussian, Σwk = 0) of the sky has the scatter σh = σ √(Σwk²). The threshold t (-th) is a false-alarm probability: a pixel of the convolved sky exceeds the threshold height with the probability Q(t) = erfc(t/√2)/2 of a Normal deviate above t. For Normal noise the height is tσh. A sky of a few photo-electrons per pixel is Poisson, its convolution is skewed (the skewness is 0.11 for FWHM 2.7 and 0.05 for 4.9 on a sky of 0.4 e⁻ with the read noise 0.37 e⁻) and has heavier tails: the height tσh used before 2026-10 passed 4–5 times more noise peaks than Normal noise (505 instead of 110 per 2048² frame at -th 4 with FWHM 2.7).

The height is computed from the noise model of the frame: Poisson photons of the mean λ = (B − BIASLEV)·GAIN electrons per pixel (at most GAIN²σ², the whole variance) and Normal noise of the rest of the variance (the read noise). The cumulant generating function of the convolution in the units of σh, K(u) = λ Σ(eu yk − 1 − u yk) + ρu²/2 with yk = wk/(GAIN σh) and ρ the Normal fraction of the variance, is inverted by the saddle-point approximation of Barndorff-Nielsen: P(h > x) = Q(r*), r* = r + ln(q/r)/r, r = √(2(ux − K)), q = u√K″(u), K′(u) = x; the threshold height x has r* = t. By an exponentially tilted Monte Carlo the tail is within 1.3% of Q(t) at t = 4 and 5 for skies of 0.13–200 e⁻ per pixel (the Cornish–Fisher expansion by the skewness and the kurtosis overcorrects up to five times below 1 e⁻). On a sky of 0.53 e⁻ (0.4 e⁻ and 0.13 e⁻ of the dark current) the height is 4.59σh for -th 4 and FWHM 2.7, 4.27σh for FWHM 4.9; on ground-based frames (hundreds of electrons) it is within 0.03σh of t. Without GAIN the noise is taken Normal (tσh, as before); BIASLEV is the pedestal of the frame (0 when missing; λ is then bounded by the variance). A stack with the GAIN of a single frame in its header gets a too high threshold.

The expected number of the noise peaks above the threshold, FALSERAT, is the lower of the number of pixels above t (Bonferroni) and the expected Euler characteristic of the excursion set of a Gaussian random field, Aλ2te−t²/2/(2π)3/2 in the area A (Adler 1981; Worsley et al. 1996, Hum. Brain Mapp. 4, 58), λ2 = 2(1 − ρ1) the variance of the derivative of the normalized convolution by the kernel, ρ1 the correlation of neighbouring pixels. The convolution by the small kernel of find is rough, and the number of pixels is the better estimate up to FWHM 10 (120–135 peaks against 132 at t = 4 on 2048² Normal noise; the number is an upper estimate). --false-rate N sets t by the expected number N of noise detections per frame: --false-rate 1 is t = 5.04 on 2048² frames (0.2–1.8 noise detections per frame for the skies above), --false-rate 10 is 4.58 (7–13).

Shape tests

The sharpness, (d0 − the mean of the other pixels of the circle of the kernel)/h, and the roundness, 2(hx − hy)/(hx + hy) of the heights of the marginal fits, tell stars from hot pixels and bad columns; the fixed windows 0.2…1.0 and −1…1 are DAOPHOT's. A faint star scatters out of them by the noise of its pixels: on QUVIK-like frames (GAIN 0.101) the photon noise of the star dominates, and 39% of the stars at 4–5σh, 28% at 8–11σh and 13% at 25–50σh were outside 0.2…1.0. Both statistics are linear in the pixels over a height; their variances are, to the first order, sums of the squared coefficients times the noise of each pixel: the sky and the photon noise of a point source of the measured height (by GAIN; a Gaussian of the FWHM integrated over the pixels and averaged over its sub-pixel position). The predicted scatter matches the observed one within 7% above 8σh.

A candidate passes when the statistic is within the fixed window, or within --shape-sigma k (default 3) times its standard deviation of the value expected for a star: the median over the bright candidates (above 10σh, at least 20, the median within the fixed window), else the model (0.40 for FWHM 4.9, 0.53 for 2.7). The sharpness stays within −1…2 (a single hot pixel has 1.4 for FWHM 2.7 and 2.7 for 4.9). In the light of a brighter star's wing (above the noise of a sky pixel) the fixed windows hold. Windows given by --shrplo,--shrphi or --rndlo,--rndhi are fixed (the old tests); --shape-sigma 0 keeps the fixed windows only. Noise peaks are not told from faint stars by their shape: the false detections are set by the threshold.

Relation to DAOPHOT II

The routine is on base of original code of DAOPHOT II by P.B.Stetson (has been forked from Midas's implementation at 1996). Some additional enhancements has been done:

  • Robust estimators replaces the original estimators of averages; the sky of the frame is the clipped mean by the noise model (2026).
  • The candidates in the wings of brighter stars are tested against the wing (2026).
  • The threshold is the false-alarm probability of the skewed (Poisson) noise of the sky; --false-rate (2026).
  • The shape tests accept the noise of faint stars (2026).
  • All processing is done in memory (no disc buffering is used).
  • FITS files for both input and output are used
  • Log format is changed.
  • Seldom bugs are corrected.
  • Code is updated for Fortran 95 (and perhaps above).

Input And Output

On input, list of frames with already applied photometric pre-calibrations is expected.

On output, a table with detected stars, represented by FITS extension, is added to all input images.

Warning. The saturation and the read-noise parameters can be specified; their values are kept in header of the new FITS extension. If the same values are also included in the original image header, they will be untouched having consequence that the values will be distinct. Munipack next processing (mainly photometry) will get the new values.

Parameters

Important:

-f
Set FWHM in pixels. The value is set by default to 3 pixels which will satisfactory for small telescopes. Hubble space telescope requires values below 1 pixel and a well sampled image has FWHM between 5 - 10 pixels.
-th
Threshold in sigmas above background (default 7): the false-alarm probability of a Normal deviate above the threshold, for the skewed noise of a faint sky too (see Threshold). One affects how much and how faint stars will be detected. About 5 (or --false-rate 1) gives one false detection per 4·10⁶ pixels, 4 about 130. Brigh stars detection only requires the threshold above 10. Checking values below sky noise is not recommended.
--false-rate
The threshold by the expected number of noise detections per frame (instead of -th).
--saturate
Saturation in ADU. The value is determined from FITS header using SATURATE key, when the keyword is not found the upper-limit of float numbers in single precision is supplied (approximate 1038). The value will usually not satisfactory because the saturation is primary given by electrical capacity of pixels (device manufacturers supplies theirs values as the full well capacity) and one is usually not related to a numerical range.

Searching Limits:

--lothresh
lower for threshold in sigmas
--shape-sigma
sharp and round within their noise in sigmas (default 3; 0: the fixed limits only)
--rndlo
lower for round (default −1; when given, the limits of round are fixed)
--rndhi
higher for round (default 1)
--shrplo
lower for sharp (default 0.2; when given, the limits of sharp are fixed)
--shrphi
higher for sharp (default 1)

Additional:

--read-noise
Read noise in ADU. A value from frame header is used by default. Use this option when the value is missing or needs correction. Read noise is important for determining of photometry errors; in find, it enters the noise model of the sky clipping only (the threshold is by the observed scatter).

See Common options for input/output filenames.

When options for the saturation or read noise are specified, FITS header is updated according to the passed value.

Examples

Detect stars on image: 

$ munipack find -f 2 -th 10 pleiades.fits

Detect stars with one noise detection per frame expected, and print the threshold: 

$ munipack find -f 4.9 --false-rate 1 --verbose frame.fits
