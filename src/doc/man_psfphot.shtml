PSF Photometry

PSF photometry fits every detected star by a point spread function (PSF): an analytic one (Gauss, Moffat), the field-dependent PSF given by a file (the same one which simulates frames in artificial), or an empirical PSF built from the stars of the frame. Close stars are fitted simultaneously in groups, and stars missed by find can be detected in the residuals.

Synopsis

munipack psfphot [.. parameters ..] file(s)

The frames must contain the FIND extension (find); the APERPHOT extension (aphot) gives the sky of stars. A new PSFPHOT extension is added to the frames.

Algorithm

  • The detector parameters are read from the header: the saturation (SATURATE; by default 2BITPIX-1 for integer frames), the gain (GAIN [e-/ADU]; 1 when it is missing or when BUNIT is COUNTS, the frames converted to electrons by phcorr), the read noise (READNS [ADU], or RDNOISE [e-] written by artificial, divided by the gain) and the bias (BIASLEV [ADU], 0 when missing). The keywords for saturation, read noise and gain can be redefined by environment variables FITS_KEY_SATURATE, FITS_KEY_READNOISE, FITS_KEY_GAIN.
  • Pixels at or above the saturation, or below LOWBAD of the FIND extension, are excluded (the same rule as find and aphot).
  • Saturated stars are not detected by find. Every connected area of saturated pixels without a detected star is added as a star (at its centroid, FLAG 64) so that it is fitted from its wings and subtracted from its neighbours; the fitting radius of saturated stars extends 1.5 FWHM beyond the saturated area. The added stars are appended after the rows of the FIND extension.
  • The PSF is prepared: an analytic PSF of the given FWHM, or the PSF with the width (and Moffat β, ellipticity) fitted on up to thirty bright (S/N > 50), isolated, unsaturated stars in the radius of 3 FWHM (the medians of their fits are used), or the PSF by the file, or the empirical PSF (see below).
  • The initial fluxes are fitted at the detected positions; the stars are ordered by their brightness.
  • Every star is fitted for its flux, position (and the sky with --sky=local) by the weighted least squares (Levenberg-Marquardt) in the circle of the fitting radius. The weights are inverse variances predicted by the model, not by the data,

    σ2 = (m - b)/g + r2 [ADU2],

    where m is the model of the pixel (the sky plus all stars), b the bias, g the gain and r the read noise in ADU (1/12 ADU2 of the quantisation is added for integer frames). The weights are iterated with the model, so faint stars are not biased (the estimate is the Poisson maximum likelihood without read noise).
  • The fitted neighbours are subtracted: the stars are fitted from the brightest, the models of the already fitted stars are subtracted from the data. The next passes (three in total, by default) refit every star with all others subtracted. The sum of the models is kept in an image, so the time is proportional to the number of stars.
  • Stars closer than the link distance (--group, 1.5 FWHM by default) are fitted simultaneously: the fluxes and positions of all members of a group (and a common sky with --sky=local) in the union of their fitting circles, the other stars subtracted. The groups are formed before every pass by the current positions (a chain of close stars is one group). A group larger than fifteen stars is split by shorter links (0.8 times, repeatedly); the stars left alone are fitted alone. The members too faint for a position, or running away, are fitted at their detected positions (FLAG 4), as single stars. When the normal equations of a group are singular, its members are fitted one by one. Iterative subtraction alone does not separate the stars closer than about 1.5 FWHM (their fluxes are biased by tens of per cent at 1 FWHM), the simultaneous fit does.
  • With --residual-detect n, stars are searched in the residuals (the frame with all fitted stars subtracted) after the passes, added (FLAG 128), and all stars are refitted (two passes); repeated n times. The detection is that of find: the amplitude of the lowered Gaussian of the (median) FWHM must be significant above the threshold of find (THRESH of the FIND extension), its sharpness within the limits of find (SHRPLO, SHRPHI), on a local maximum, farther than 0.75 FWHM from all stars and not on saturated areas. The significance is computed against the noise predicted by the model, with the PSF mismatch added (its relative level measured on the bright stars), and it is scaled down by the scatter of the amplitude observed in the annulus of 2-4 FWHM around the candidate when it exceeds the predicted one (the residuals of bright and saturated stars). The added stars follow the saturated stars added by psfphot in the table.
  • Stars too faint for a reliable position (S/N < 3 at the detected position), or whose fit moves by more than a half of the fitting radius (at least one pixel), are fitted for the flux only at the detected position (FLAG 4).
  • The errors are given by the covariance matrix of the fit; the error of the fixed sky (SKYERR by aphot) is propagated to the flux error.

PSF

All PSFs are sampled with ZOOM samples per pixel (16 for analytic PSFs, the value of ZOOM of a PSF file). The value of a pixel is the integral of the linearly interpolated samples (for odd ZOOM, of the samples taken as constant in their cells) over the pixel area, for any sub-pixel position of the star; the flux is conserved exactly. This is the same code which renders stars in artificial, so frames simulated with a PSF are fitted by exactly the same PSF.

GAUSS
exp(-(x²/2σ²) - (y²/2σ²q²)), FWHM = 2.3548 σ √q.
MOFFAT
(1 + x²/α² + y²/α²q²)-β, FWHM = 2α √(21/β-1) √q.
FILE
The field-dependent PSF in the PSF file format. The position in the field is given by the pixel offsets from the field centre (NAXIS/2, integer division, as in artificial; --psf-centre) divided by the scale [pix/deg] (the WCS of the frame, --scale).
EMPIRICAL
The PSF built from the stars of the frame (below).

x, y are along the major and the minor axis, the major axis is inclined by the position angle from the x-axis (counter-clockwise); q is the axis ratio (1 for circular PSF). The analytic PSFs of artificial (--hwhm is σ or α, --eccentricity e is q = √(1-e²), --inclination is the angle) are the same functions.

Empirical PSF

The empirical PSF (--psf EMPIRICAL, in the manner of DAOPHOT) is a base plus residual tables:

PSF(x,y) = B + Σt Pt(x,y) Rt,

where B is an analytic core (--psf-core, Moffat by default) with its width, β, axis ratio and angle fitted on bright isolated stars, or the PSF of a file (--psf-file, the correction mode). The tables Rt are sampled with ZOOM samples per pixel, as the core, so the empirical PSF has the same representation as a PSF file. Pt are the terms of a polynomial of the order 0, 1 or 2 (--psf-var; 1, 3 or 6 terms: 1, x, y, x², xy, y²) in the coordinates normalised to ±1 over the frame (DAOPHOT VARIABLE = 0, 1, 2).

  • The PSF stars: by a list (--psf-list; the nearest star within 2 pixels), or automatically the brightest (by S/N, at least 30) of the stars which are not saturated, have no bad pixels and no saturated star in the table radius, lie inside of the frame with the whole table, have no brighter neighbour within the table and no neighbour brighter than 10% within 3 FWHM, and have the sharpness and roundness within 4σ of the median; up to --psf-stars (100). With --verbose, the stars are listed. The stars with χ² above the median + 5σ (and 1.5 times the median) after the second iteration are not used further.
  • The tables are fitted by the weighted linear least squares on the pixels of the PSF stars within the table radius, with the exact model: the pixel of a star is the integral of the interpolated samples over the pixel at the sub-pixel position of the star (the same as for fitting and in artificial). Every star samples the grid at its own phase; the solution combines them without interpolating the data to the grid, so the result has no sub-pixel bias. The neighbours of the PSF stars are subtracted, the fluxes and positions of the PSF stars are fixed by their fits, the weights are the inverse variances of the model, outlying pixels are clipped at 5σ. A sky offset of every PSF star is fitted together with the tables, on its pixels and a ring just outside of the table radius (a quarter of it, at least 3 pixels), where the tables are zero: inside of the table a sky error cannot be told from a pedestal of the tables. A light regularisation by the squared Laplacian of the tables (--psf-smooth, relative to the mean of the diagonal of the normal matrix) keeps them smooth where few stars sample the grid. The system is solved by the preconditioned conjugate gradients.
  • The tables carry no flux and do not move the centroid: after every build they are corrected by a combination of the base and its derivatives so that their sums and first moments are zero, and the model is rescaled accordingly. The flux of the PSF is the flux of the base (the analytic core is normalised to the unit flux over the whole plane, the light beyond the table is in its wings); the centre of the PSF is the centroid of the base (for a symmetric core, the centroid of the star; in the correction mode, the convention of the file). The tables are zero outside of their radius, where no data constrain them.
  • Iterations (three): all stars are fitted by the current PSF (neighbours subtracted, groups fitted simultaneously), the tables are rebuilt; then the usual passes follow.
  • The zoom is 4 for FWHM < 5 pixels and 2 otherwise (--psf-zoom), the zoom of the file in the correction mode. The table radius is 4 FWHM (--psf-radius); in the correction mode it covers the whole stamps of the file (a halo of the field PSFs can reach far beyond 4 FWHM).
  • The variation of the order 1 or 2 requires three stars per term (9, 18); with fewer stars a lower order is used with a warning. The analytic core does not vary over the field, the tables absorb the variation of the width (as in DAOPHOT).
  • --psf-save file writes the empirical PSF in the PSF file format, so it can be used by psfphot --psf-file and artificial --psf-file. A constant PSF with an analytic core is one stamp at the field centre. A varying PSF (or a correction of a file) is sampled on rings around the field centre (NAXIS/2 or --psf-centre, the same convention as for PSF files) up to the farthest corner of the frame: 2 rings by 16 angles for the order 0 or 1, 12 rings by 32 angles for the order 2 (plus the rings of the base file inside of the frame). RADCOO is the distance in pixels divided by the scale of the frame [pix/deg] (WCS or --scale); without a scale, RADCOO is in pixels (the scale 1) and the file is used with --scale 1. The primary header records PSFCEN1, PSFCEN2, PSFSCALE, FWHM and PSFRAD. The file interpolates linearly between the rings and angles: the saved PSF of the order 2 reproduces the fluxes of the internal one to 0.05% (99% of stars), the constant one to 0.001%. The stamps contain the core out to 99.9% of its flux.

Limitations: the polynomial represents smooth variations (focus, astigmatism, coma growing over the field). A PSF which changes its structure over the field (for example a sharp core and a wide halo mixed linearly with the radius) is not a low-order polynomial; the correction mode with a model of the PSF (from the laboratory or an optics simulation) as the base is suitable for it. The wings beyond the table radius are those of the core. The PSF stars must be mostly single: in a field where most stars are unresolved blends the PSF is too broad.

Parameters

--psf GAUSS|MOFFAT|FILE|EMPIRICAL
The PSF, GAUSS by default (FILE when --psf-file is given).
--psf-file file
The PSF file (the same as for artificial --psf-file); with EMPIRICAL the base corrected by the residual tables.
--psf-core MOFFAT|GAUSS
The analytic core of the empirical PSF, MOFFAT by default: its wings carry the light beyond the table, β is fitted (up to 10, which is a Gaussian for this purpose). A Gaussian core on stars with Moffat wings loses that light (−0.9% for β = 2.5).
--psf-zoom n
The samples per pixel of the empirical PSF: 4 for FWHM < 5 pixels, 2 otherwise.
--psf-radius r
The radius of the residual tables in pixels, 4 FWHM by default.
--psf-var 0|1|2
The order of the polynomial variation over the field, 0 by default.
--psf-stars n
The maximum of the PSF stars, 100 by default.
--psf-list file
The PSF stars: x y [pix] per line (# for comments), or a FITS table (.fits, .fit, .fts) with the columns X, Y.
--psf-smooth s
The regularisation of the tables, 0.01 by default.
--psf-save file
Save the empirical PSF to the PSF file (overwritten); for a single input file.
--fwhm f
FWHM of the analytic PSF in pixels. If not given, the PSF shape is fitted (the result is printed with --verbose and written to the header). Moffat β is 2.5 when FWHM is given and β is not.
--beta β
Moffat exponent (fitted when not given, together with the width).
--elliptic
Fit also the axis ratio and the position angle of the analytic PSF.
--fitrad r
The fitting radius in pixels. The default is 1.5 FWHM (at least 3 pixels) of the local PSF.
--sky fixed|local
fixed (default): the sky in the annulus of aphot (ANNULUS1, ANNULUS2 of APERPHOT; without APERPHOT, the annulus from the fitting radius + 2 pixels, at least 5 pixels wide). The first pass uses the sky of aphot (or a median in the annulus); before the next passes, the sky is re-estimated in the annulus of the frame with all fitted stars subtracted (the mean clipped at 3σ), so the light of neighbours (and of the star itself) does not bias it. local: a constant sky is fitted for every star.
--maxiter n
The maximum of iterations of a fit, 50 by default.
--npass n
The number of passes over all stars, 3 by default.
--saturate s, --gain g, --read-noise r
Override the header: saturation [ADU], gain [e-/ADU], read noise [ADU].
--scale s, --psf-centre x,y
The scale [pix/deg] and the field centre [pix] for the PSF file.
--group d
The link distance of groups in units of FWHM, 1.5 by default; 0 fits every star alone (the iterative subtraction only, as before).
--residual-detect n
The passes of the detection of stars in the residuals, 0 by default (the output has the rows of FIND and of the saturated stars only, matching aphot and gphot; the detection costs two more passes).
--residual file
Save the frame with all fitted stars subtracted (the sky is kept); for a single input file.

See Common options for input/output filenames.

Output

The PSFPHOT table has a row for every row of the FIND extension (in the same order), followed by the added saturated stars and the stars found in the residuals:

X, Y, XERR, YERR
The fitted position and its errors [pix] (the errors are -1 when the position is not fitted).
PSFCOUNT, PSFCOUNTERR
The total flux of the star (the integral of the PSF) and its error in the units of the frame (ADU), the same quantity as GCOUNT of gphot or aperture-corrected APCOUNT. PSFCOUNTERR is -1 when the fit failed.
SKY, SKYERR
The sky and its error [ADU].
CHI2
The reduced χ² of the fit (about 1 for a good fit).
SHARP
The relative residual in the core (inside the half of FWHM): positive for objects sharper than PSF (cosmic rays, hot pixels), negative for extended ones (galaxies, blends).
ROUND
The ellipticity of the data minus that of the model.
NPIX
The number of fitted pixels (in the fitting circle of the star).
FLAG
The sum of: 1 saturated pixel(s) in the core (the flux is estimated from the wings only), 2 pixels in the fitting radius excluded (bad, saturated, out of the frame), 4 the position is not fitted, 8 not converged, 16 the fit failed (the flux is not valid), 32 another star within the fitting radius, 64 a saturated star added (not detected by find), 128 a star found in the residuals.
GROUP, GSIZE
The group of the simultaneous fit in the last pass (0 for a star fitted alone) and its size.

The header keywords: PSFTYPE, PSFFILE, FWHM, HWHM [pix], PSFSIGMA or PSFALPHA and PSFBETA, PSFAXRAT, PSFPA [deg], PSFNSTAR (stars of the shape fit), PSFCEN1, PSFCEN2, PSFSCALE (the PSF file geometry), PSFZOOM, FITRAD, SKYMODE, NPASS, GROUPDST (the link distance [FWHM]), NGROUPS, MAXGROUP, RESDETEC, NRESID (stars found in the residuals) and the detector parameters used for the weights: GAIN, READNS, BIASLEV, SATURATE. The empirical PSF adds PSFCORE (GAUSS, MOFFAT, FILE), PSFVAR, PSFNSTAR (the PSF stars), PSFNSHAP (stars of the core fit), PSFRAD [pix], PSFRESRM (the rms of the constant table relative to the peak of the core), PSFCHI2 (the median χ² of the PSF stars), PSFITER and PSFSAVED; FWHM is that of the empirical PSF at the field centre and PSFSIGMA (PSFALPHA, PSFBETA), PSFAXRAT, PSFPA describe the core.

phcal calibrates the PSF photometry with --photometry=psf.

Examples

PSF photometry with a Gaussian PSF of fitted width:

$ munipack find frame.fits
$ munipack aphot frame.fits
$ munipack psfphot --verbose frame.fits

The field-dependent PSF which simulated the frames:

$ munipack artificial --psf-file psf.fits -c cat.fits ...
$ munipack find artificial.fits
$ munipack aphot artificial.fits
$ munipack psfphot --psf-file psf.fits --residual res.fits artificial.fits
$ munipack astrometry -c cat.fits artificial.fits
$ munipack phcal --photometry=psf -c cat.fits ... artificial.fits

The empirical PSF, varying to the second order over the field, saved for a later use (and the simulation):

$ munipack psfphot --psf EMPIRICAL --psf-var 2 --psf-save epsf.fits --verbose frame.fits
$ munipack psfphot --psf-file epsf.fits other.fits
$ munipack artificial --psf-file epsf.fits -c cat.fits ...

A laboratory PSF corrected by the stars of the frame, the frame searched for the stars missed by find:

$ munipack psfphot --psf EMPIRICAL --psf-file lab.fits --residual-detect 1 frame.fits

Limitations

  • Stars closer than about 0.5 FWHM are not separated (find, and the detection in the residuals, see one star).
  • The polynomial of the empirical PSF does not represent PSFs which change their structure over the field (see Empirical PSF).
  • The time is dominated by sampling and pixelising the PSF again for every fit of every star (no cache of PSFs, a single thread).
