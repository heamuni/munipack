PSF Photometry

PSF photometry fits every detected star by a point spread function (PSF): an analytic one (Gauss, Moffat), or the field-dependent PSF given by a file, the same one which simulates frames in artificial.

Synopsis

munipack psfphot [.. parameters ..] file(s)

The frames must contain the FIND extension (find); the APERPHOT extension (aphot) gives the sky of stars. A new PSFPHOT extension is added to the frames.

Algorithm

  • The detector parameters are read from the header: the saturation (SATURATE; by default 2BITPIX-1 for integer frames), the gain (GAIN [e-/ADU]; 1 when it is missing or when BUNIT is COUNTS, the frames converted to electrons by phcorr), the read noise (READNS [ADU], or RDNOISE [e-] written by artificial, divided by the gain) and the bias (BIASLEV [ADU], 0 when missing). The keywords for saturation, read noise and gain can be redefined by environment variables FITS_KEY_SATURATE, FITS_KEY_READNOISE, FITS_KEY_GAIN.
  • Pixels at or above the saturation, or below LOWBAD of the FIND extension, are excluded (the same rule as find and aphot).
  • The PSF is prepared: an analytic PSF of the given FWHM, or the PSF with the width (and Moffat β, ellipticity) fitted on up to thirty bright (S/N > 50), isolated, unsaturated stars in the radius of 3 FWHM (the medians of their fits are used), or the PSF by the file.
  • The initial fluxes are fitted at the detected positions; the stars are ordered by their brightness.
  • Every star is fitted for its flux, position (and the sky with --sky=local) by the weighted least squares (Levenberg-Marquardt) in the circle of the fitting radius. The weights are inverse variances predicted by the model, not by the data,

    σ2 = (m - b)/g + r2 [ADU2],

    where m is the model of the pixel (the sky plus all stars), b the bias, g the gain and r the read noise in ADU (1/12 ADU2 of the quantisation is added for integer frames). The weights are iterated with the model, so faint stars are not biased (the estimate is the Poisson maximum likelihood without read noise).
  • The fitted neighbours are subtracted: the stars are fitted from the brightest, the models of the already fitted stars are subtracted from the data. The next passes (three in total, by default) refit every star with all others subtracted. The sum of the models is kept in an image, so the time is proportional to the number of stars.
  • Stars too faint for a reliable position (S/N < 3 at the detected position), or whose fit moves by more than a half of the fitting radius (at least one pixel), are fitted for the flux only at the detected position (FLAG 4).
  • The errors are given by the covariance matrix of the fit; the error of the fixed sky (SKYERR by aphot) is propagated to the flux error.

PSF

All PSFs are sampled with ZOOM samples per pixel (16 for analytic PSFs, the value of ZOOM of a PSF file). The value of a pixel is the integral of the linearly interpolated samples (for odd ZOOM, of the samples taken as constant in their cells) over the pixel area, for any sub-pixel position of the star; the flux is conserved exactly. This is the same code which renders stars in artificial, so frames simulated with a PSF are fitted by exactly the same PSF.

GAUSS
exp(-(x²/2σ²) - (y²/2σ²q²)), FWHM = 2.3548 σ √q.
MOFFAT
(1 + x²/α² + y²/α²q²)-β, FWHM = 2α √(21/β-1) √q.
FILE
The field-dependent PSF in the PSF file format. The position in the field is given by the pixel offsets from the field centre (NAXIS/2, integer division, as in artificial; --psf-centre) divided by the scale [pix/deg] (the WCS of the frame, --scale).

x, y are along the major and the minor axis, the major axis is inclined by the position angle from the x-axis (counter-clockwise); q is the axis ratio (1 for circular PSF). The analytic PSFs of artificial (--hwhm is σ or α, --eccentricity e is q = √(1-e²), --inclination is the angle) are the same functions.

Parameters

--psf GAUSS|MOFFAT|FILE
The PSF, GAUSS by default (FILE when --psf-file is given).
--psf-file file
The PSF file (the same as for artificial --psf-file).
--fwhm f
FWHM of the analytic PSF in pixels. If not given, the PSF shape is fitted (the result is printed with --verbose and written to the header). Moffat β is 2.5 when FWHM is given and β is not.
--beta β
Moffat exponent (fitted when not given, together with the width).
--elliptic
Fit also the axis ratio and the position angle of the analytic PSF.
--fitrad r
The fitting radius in pixels. The default is 1.5 FWHM (at least 3 pixels) of the local PSF.
--sky fixed|local
fixed (default): the sky in the annulus of aphot (ANNULUS1, ANNULUS2 of APERPHOT; without APERPHOT, the annulus from the fitting radius + 2 pixels, at least 5 pixels wide). The first pass uses the sky of aphot (or a median in the annulus); before the next passes, the sky is re-estimated in the annulus of the frame with all fitted stars subtracted (the mean clipped at 3σ), so the light of neighbours (and of the star itself) does not bias it. local: a constant sky is fitted for every star.
--maxiter n
The maximum of iterations of a fit, 50 by default.
--npass n
The number of passes over all stars, 3 by default.
--saturate s, --gain g, --read-noise r
Override the header: saturation [ADU], gain [e-/ADU], read noise [ADU].
--scale s, --psf-centre x,y
The scale [pix/deg] and the field centre [pix] for the PSF file.
--residual file
Save the frame with all fitted stars subtracted (the sky is kept); for a single input file.

See Common options for input/output filenames.

Output

The PSFPHOT table has a row for every row of the FIND extension:

X, Y, XERR, YERR
The fitted position and its errors [pix] (the errors are -1 when the position is not fitted).
PSFCOUNT, PSFCOUNTERR
The total flux of the star (the integral of the PSF) and its error in the units of the frame (ADU), the same quantity as GCOUNT of gphot or aperture-corrected APCOUNT. PSFCOUNTERR is -1 when the fit failed.
SKY, SKYERR
The sky and its error [ADU].
CHI2
The reduced χ² of the fit (about 1 for a good fit).
SHARP
The relative residual in the core (inside the half of FWHM): positive for objects sharper than PSF (cosmic rays, hot pixels), negative for extended ones (galaxies, blends).
ROUND
The ellipticity of the data minus that of the model.
NPIX
The number of fitted pixels.
FLAG
The sum of: 1 saturated pixel(s) in the core (the flux is estimated from the wings only), 2 pixels in the fitting radius excluded (bad, saturated, out of the frame), 4 the position is not fitted, 8 not converged, 16 the fit failed (the flux is not valid), 32 another star within the fitting radius.

The header keywords: PSFTYPE, PSFFILE, FWHM, HWHM [pix], PSFSIGMA or PSFALPHA and PSFBETA, PSFAXRAT, PSFPA [deg], PSFNSTAR (stars of the shape fit), PSFCEN1, PSFCEN2, PSFSCALE (the PSF file geometry), PSFZOOM, FITRAD, SKYMODE, NPASS and the detector parameters used for the weights: GAIN, READNS, BIASLEV, SATURATE.

phcal calibrates the PSF photometry with --photometry=psf.

Examples

PSF photometry with a Gaussian PSF of fitted width:

$ munipack find frame.fits
$ munipack aphot frame.fits
$ munipack psfphot --verbose frame.fits

The field-dependent PSF which simulated the frames:

$ munipack artificial --psf-file psf.fits -c cat.fits ...
$ munipack find artificial.fits
$ munipack aphot artificial.fits
$ munipack psfphot --psf-file psf.fits --residual res.fits artificial.fits
$ munipack astrometry -c cat.fits artificial.fits
$ munipack phcal --photometry=psf -c cat.fits ... artificial.fits

Limitations

  • Stars are fitted one by one (with neighbours subtracted), not in groups; very close pairs (below about one FWHM) are not separated reliably.
  • Only stars detected by find are fitted, no new stars are searched in the residuals.
  • The PSF file is used as it is; an empirical PSF is not built from the frame.
